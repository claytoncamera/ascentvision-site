What is a conversion rate, and how do you improve it on a service website?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

A conversion rate is the share of visitors who take the action you want, which for a service business is usually getting in touch. If 1,000 people visit and 20 send an inquiry, the conversion rate is 2%. It is improved by making the site clearer, faster and easier to act on, and judged by real inquiries, not by clicks.

How to measure it honestly

  • Decide what counts: a submitted form, a call, a booking, or all three.
  • Count real inquiries, not button clicks or page views.
  • Compare like with like: the same pages, the same time of year.
  • Look at inquiries that became customers too; a higher rate of poor-fit inquiries is not progress.

Changes that usually help

  • Say what you do, where and for whom in the first screen.
  • Ask for one action, with the same button wording everywhere.
  • Keep the form short. Nielsen Norman Group's form guidance is to keep it short and remove fields you can get another way.
  • Show real proof near the button: real reviews, real work.
  • Make sure the page is quick and easy to use on a phone.
  • Say how fast you reply, and then keep that promise.

Test one change at a time

Change one thing, wait long enough to see a difference in real inquiries, and keep what works. On a small site with few visitors, judge by the trend over weeks rather than by a single day.

Sources

  1. Kathryn Whitenton, "Website Forms Usability: Top 10 Recommendations", Nielsen Norman Group, May 1, 2016
