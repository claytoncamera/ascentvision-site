Can you build it with no-code tools, or do you need custom software?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

No-code and low-code tools let someone build forms, simple workflows and internal lists by arranging pieces on a screen, with little or no code. They often suit a first version or a task with few users and standard rules. They can strain with many users, unusual rules, connections to other tools and data you must keep. Before you start, check that you can export your data, that the account is in your name, what the limits are and how the price grows with users.

What they do well

These strengths are usually clearest when few people use the tool and the rules are standard.

  • Forms that send answers to a list or a person.
  • Simple steps, such as an approval, or a reminder when something changes.
  • Internal lists and trackers that a few staff keep.
  • A quick first version to see whether people use the idea, in line with the related answer on a minimum viable product.
  • Changes made by you or a staff member without waiting for a developer.

Where they can strain

Someone has to maintain whatever gets built. If the one person who understands it is away or leaves, the business can be stuck.

  • Many users, or different views and sign-in rules for staff, customers and managers.
  • Rules with many exceptions, which can grow into a tangle of steps only the builder understands.
  • Connections to tools the platform does not support, or that its plan leaves out.
  • Data you must keep safe or move, such as customer, payment or health details.
  • A polished customer-facing app that must behave the same on every phone.

Checks before you start

CheckWhat to find out
Data exportCan you take out everything, including history and files, in a form another tool can open? Try an export in the first week, and see the related answer on switching business software for checking one.
Whose accountIs it opened with a business email in the business's name and with more than one administrator, not on one person's own login? Who else can see the data?
LimitsWhat the plan caps (records, users, automatic runs, storage) and what happens at the cap: does work stop, slow down or cost more?
Price as you growHow the price changes as users or runs are added, and what the next plan up includes. Work from your use a year from now, not today.
Changes by the vendorAsk how much notice customers get, and keep the export current.
ConnectionsWhich of your other tools it links to, who fixes a link that breaks and what the link can see.

When to move on

Moving on does not mean the first build was wasted. Its screens, rules and real examples are a good start for a developer's brief; the related answer on what to have ready before hiring a developer lists what to write down. The related answer on custom or off-the-shelf software helps compare buying a product with having one built. Some jobs may never need to move. Ascent Vision builds custom software, so read this with that in mind.

  • You spend more effort working around the tool than using it.
  • Staff keep a second spreadsheet because the tool cannot hold something.
  • The price rises faster than the value as users are added.
  • A rule you need cannot be expressed, or mistakes reach customers or money.
