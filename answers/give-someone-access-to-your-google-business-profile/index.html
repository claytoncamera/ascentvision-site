How do you give an employee or agency access to your Google Business Profile without giving up ownership?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Invite them from People and access in your profile's settings, using their own Google Account, and choose Manager rather than Owner. Google says managers have mostly the same access as owners, such as editing info, replying to reviews and posting, but cannot add or remove users or remove the profile. Keep primary ownership yourself, and when someone leaves, remove them from the same screen.

Owners, the primary owner and managers

Google says a profile can have several owners but only one primary owner, and only the primary owner can transfer that role. Managers, once called site managers, have almost the same access as owners. Some rows from Google's comparison:

OwnerManager
Add and remove usersYesNo
Remove the profileYesNo
Edit the name, category or website, or close a locationYesYes
Edit hours, address and phone numberYesYes
Reply to reviews and publish postsYesYes
Manage Google Ads account linksYesYes

How to add someone

Google says each person needs their own Google Account, so no one has to share your password, and that Google Groups cannot be added. Its steps:

  • Go to your Business Profile and select More Business Profile settings, then People and access.
  • At the top left, select Add and enter the person's email address.
  • Under Access, choose Owner or Manager, then select Invite.
  • The person can accept and become a user right away, and you get an email when they do. Pending invites are listed under PENDING on the same page, where you can cancel one.

Which role to give

As general advice, not Google's: give staff and outside help the Manager role, and keep Owner for the people who own the business. A manager can still rename the profile or close a location, so even that role is for someone you trust. Google's ownership guidelines say an agency must, whenever possible, encourage the owner to own the profile and add the agency as a manager; the related answer on what to do if someone else controls your Google Business Profile covers the rest of those rules.

Google says a profile needs at least one other owner or manager before primary ownership can be transferred. As general advice, a second owner from inside the business, such as a partner, means one lost login does not lock everyone out; the related answer on who should own your ad accounts makes the same case for ads.

When someone leaves

In People and access, select the person, then Remove person. Google says only owners can remove owners and managers, that the person removed gets an email, and that their past review replies and posts stay on the profile.

If the person leaving is the primary owner, Google says they cannot remove themselves until they transfer primary ownership: in People and access, select the new primary owner, select Edit next to Access, choose Primary owner and save. Google also says someone added in the last 7 days cannot yet make anyone primary owner. When an agency leaves, the related answer on how to switch marketing agencies lists the other accounts to check.
