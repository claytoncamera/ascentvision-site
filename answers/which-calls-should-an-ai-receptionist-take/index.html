Which calls should an AI receptionist take, and which should a person?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

An AI receptionist can usually take simple questions and bookings within rules you set, and take a message after hours. Emergencies, complaints, upset callers, price negotiation and anything legal or medical should go to a person, or at least be passed on at once with the caller's details. Write the hand-off rules down before it answers a live call.

Sorting calls by type

Simple questionsAnswer from facts you wrote, such as hours and service areaTake anything not on that list

Rules for handing a call to a person

  • The caller asks for a person, in any words. It should connect them or take a message, not argue.
  • The caller seems upset, repeats themselves or says they are not being understood.
  • The call mentions smoke, flooding, injury or another safety risk. As general advice, the script can tell callers who are in danger to call 911 first.
  • The caller asks for a discount, a firm promise about timing or any other promise.
  • It touches the law, an injury, a refund dispute or health advice.
  • The system is unsure. It should say it will pass the question on, not guess.

Make the hand-off work

A hand-off only helps if someone is there. Decide who is on call, how they are alerted, how long they have to respond, and what the system tells the caller if nobody answers. A caller who is told "someone will call you" with no time and no follow-through is likely to try another business.

Whoever picks up should see the caller's name, number, what they said and when they called, so they do not ask everything again. Read the summaries daily at first, and treat a call that should have gone to a person but did not as a reason to tighten the rules. Any callback or text goes out only with the person's consent.
