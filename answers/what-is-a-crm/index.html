What is a CRM, and does a small business need one?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

A CRM (customer relationship management system) is one shared place that records every customer and inquiry: who they are, how they found you, what was said and what happens next. A small business does not always need dedicated software. A well-kept spreadsheet or shared board often works at first. A CRM earns its place when inquiries start slipping through, follow-ups get missed, or more than one person handles leads.

What a CRM holds

  • Contact details for each person who gets in touch.
  • Where the inquiry came from: search, AI answer, social, referral, paid, email, campaign or direct.
  • Every call, email and message, with dates.
  • The next step, when it is due and who owns it.
  • The outcome: won, lost or still open, and why.

When a spreadsheet or shared board is enough

If one person handles every inquiry, and there are few enough to see on one screen, a spreadsheet or a shared board is usually enough. What matters is the habit, not the tool: one row per inquiry, a source column, a next-step date and an outcome.

A spreadsheet kept up every day usually beats software nobody opens.

Signs you have outgrown it

  • An inquiry was forgotten and the customer hired someone else.
  • Two people called the same customer, or nobody did.
  • You cannot say where last month's jobs came from.
  • Follow-up depends on someone remembering.
  • Notes live in personal phones and inboxes, so they leave when a person leaves.

What to look for

  • You own the data, and you can export all of it, including notes and history, in a common format such as a spreadsheet file.
  • It works well on a phone, because much of the follow-up happens away from a desk.
  • Every record has a place for the source and the outcome.
  • It reminds people of next steps without being asked.
  • It is simple enough that everyone actually uses it. A few features used daily beat many features ignored.

A good first step

Before choosing any software, write down the few fields you need to track and run them in a spreadsheet for a month. If it holds up, keep it. If it breaks, you will know exactly what a CRM has to do for you, which makes choosing one much easier.
