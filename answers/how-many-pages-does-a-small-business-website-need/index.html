How many pages does a small business website need?

Ascent Vision · Published October 18, 2026

Most small service businesses do well with four to seven pages: a homepage, a page for each main service customers hire you for, an about page and a contact page. A brand-new business with one service in one area can start with a single good page and grow from there. Add a page only when you have something real to put on it; a thin, empty page helps no one, and the count itself wins nothing.

A starter list for a service business

PageWhat it is for
HomepageWhat you do, where, and for whom, with one clear next step
One page per main serviceWhat is included, who it suits, and examples of real jobs
AboutWho is behind the business and why they can be trusted
ContactEvery way to reach you, and what happens after someone does

When one page is enough

A new business with one service and one area can start with a single page that does the homepage's job well: what you do, where, for whom, some proof, and an easy way to make contact. One clear page usually beats five thin ones. The related answers on what a service business homepage should say and how to describe your services cover what belongs on these pages.

When a service earns its own page

Split a service onto its own page when customers ask about it on its own and you have enough to say: what is included, how the work usually goes, photos of real jobs, and the questions people ask before hiring. If all you could write is a heading and two sentences, leave it as a section on an existing page until there is more.

What matters more than the count

Do not add pages to look bigger or to chase every phrase someone might type. A visitor who lands on an empty page leaves, and the inquiry leaves with them. Pages exist to answer a customer's question or to make contact easier; a page that does neither can be cut without loss.
