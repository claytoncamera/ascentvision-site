How do plumbers get more leads?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Plumbers usually get more leads by answering the emergency call, because an urgent customer often takes whoever replies first. Local Services Ads are one paid option; a complete Google profile, past customers and fairly asked reviews cost only time. In Florida, the statute puts a contractor's license number in offers and advertisements. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

The after-hours call is the lead

A burst pipe or a backed-up drain does not wait for office hours, so the call or message at night is often the lead itself. The related answer on after-hours inquiries covers the quick acknowledgement and the exact time of the real reply, and the related answer on reply speed explains why the first hour matters. For a plumber, add one step: decide ahead what counts as an emergency, and say so on the profile and the voicemail.

Paid options: Local Services Ads and lead sites

Google's page on getting started with Local Services Ads lists Plumbers among its categories, and the account tools it lists include providing lead feedback and tracking lead credits. The related answer on what Google Local Services Ads are covers how the ads work, the screening and the per-lead billing.

Lead sites are the other paid route. The related answer on buying leads from lead sites says to judge each one by the jobs you win from it, not by the leads it sends. The same test works for Local Services Ads.

The license number

Section 489.105 of the 2026 Florida Statutes lists plumbing contractor among the contractor types, and section 489.119 puts a contractor's number in offers, bids, contracts and advertisements; the related answer on showing a license number and insurance covers that rule and what it does and does not say.

Whether a particular web page or profile counts as an advertisement is a matter for the board's rule, so a plumber with a doubt can ask the board or a Florida attorney. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Free routes cost time

Free routes still cost time, and three of them cost little else. First, the Google Business Profile: Google's guidelines page uses a plumber who runs a business from a residential address as its example of a business that should clear the address from its profile, and its service-area page allows up to 20 service areas. Second, past customers: a short, honest note to people you have already served, by text only with the person's consent; the related answer on winning back past customers has the wording. Third, reviews: the related answer on getting reviews the right way says to ask every customer when the work is done, with a direct link, and to give nothing in return.
