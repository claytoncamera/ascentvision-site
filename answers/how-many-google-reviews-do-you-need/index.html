How many Google reviews does a business need?

Ascent Vision · Published October 26, 2026

Google's local ranking tips name no target number. It says more reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking. Customers set their own bar: in BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 U.S. adults, 47% said they won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and only 9% were willing to use one with five or fewer. Recency counts too, so a steady flow of reviews from every customer usually beats a burst collected once.

What Google says

Google's tips on local ranking say local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity, and describe prominence, how well known a business is, as based partly on information such as how many reviews you have. Its words: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." The same page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Google's page on review scores says the score is the average of all ratings published on Google for the business. The related answer on how Google works out your star rating shows why each new review moves a small profile more than a large one.

What customers say

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, of 1,002 U.S. adults, found that:

  • 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and only 9% are willing to use one with five or fewer.
  • 74% seek reviews written in the last three months, and 18% are only swayed by reviews written within the last week.
  • 31% will only use a business that has 4.5 stars or more.

A target you can keep up

As general advice, aim for a habit rather than a number: ask every customer after every job, so new reviews keep arriving. A first goal of about 20 matches the bar many people in the survey set. Beyond that, compare yourself with the businesses that show up near you for the same service, and keep your newest review recent. The related answer on getting more reviews the right way covers how to ask.

What not to do

Google's Maps content policy does not allow merchants to offer incentives, such as payment, discounts or free services, in exchange for a review, or to selectively solicit positive reviews. Never buy reviews or write your own; the related answer on whether online reviews matter summarizes the FTC's rule on fake reviews.
