Should you boost a Facebook post or run an ad?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Both are paid ads. Meta describes a boosted post as an ad made from something you already posted, and calls it the simplest way to get a post seen by more people. Ads built in Meta's Ads Manager add more choices, including objectives such as leads. For a service business, boosting often suits getting a strong post seen nearby, and a full ad often suits collecting inquiries. Judge either one by the inquiries and jobs it brings, not by likes.

What Meta says a boosted post is

Meta's Business Help Center article "The difference between boosted posts and Meta ads" says a boosted post is an ad you create from a post, Story or Reel you already published on your Facebook Page or Instagram account. When you boost, you choose a goal, such as more messages, more engagement or more website visitors, along with the ad text, the schedule, the budget and where it appears.

The same article says boosted posts are still ads, because they need a budget to reach a wider audience, and that a boosted post shows on your bill as an ad. Meta's article "Boost a post from your Facebook Page" adds that you can include another way for customers to contact you after they open the ad.

What a full ad adds

Meta's article compares the two. This table puts its points side by side.

Boosted postAd made in Ads Manager
Starts fromA post you already publishedAn ad you design for the goal
Goals Meta namesWebsite clicks, Page engagement and local business promotionsAlso leads, app promotion and sales
TargetingInterests, age and genderMore advanced tools, such as lookalike audiences
FormatsThe post, with optional text variationsFormats boosting does not offer, such as carousel ads

Which suits a service business

Meta's article says boosting is a good way to get engagement on your Page or build awareness of your brand, and points to Meta Business Suite or Ads Manager for more advanced ad types. The rest of this section is general advice, not Meta's.

  • Boosting often makes sense when a post is already doing well with people who know you, such as photos of a finished job, and you want more people nearby to see it. Choosing messages as the goal can turn some of that attention into inquiries.
  • A full ad often makes sense when the goal is inquiries you can follow up, such as a form a customer fills in. The related answer on Facebook and Instagram lead ads covers that format.
  • Boosting a post that did not interest your own followers rarely fixes it. Start from your best post, not your newest.

Judge it by inquiries, not likes

Likes and reach are easy to see but say little about jobs. Record every inquiry that came from a boosted post or an ad as paid, with the platform in the notes, even when it arrives as a message, and follow each to won, lost or still open. The related answer on Facebook and Instagram lead ads uses the same test, and the related answer on tracking social media messages covers the inbox side. Call or text a lead only with the person's consent.
