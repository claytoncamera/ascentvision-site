What should you do about duplicate Google listings or a second Google profile for your business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

First work out whether it is a true duplicate. Google allows one Business Profile per business and says a duplicate will not show on Search or Maps, but a second location with its own staff and service area, or a distinct business at your address, can have its own. A copy you made by mistake can be removed by its owner, and two Maps listings for one business can be reported for a merge, which Google reviews.

A true duplicate or an allowed second profile

Google's help page on duplicates says a profile may be treated as a duplicate when a verified profile already exists for the same business, when several profiles share an address, or when one business has separate profiles for different services. Google's pages also describe cases where a second profile is allowed:

SituationWhat Google's pages say
Another location of a service businessAllowed, one profile per location, when each has separate service areas and separate staff
A different business at your addressAllowed if both are distinct and eligible, with different names and clearly visible differences in signage
Individual practitionersPublic-facing professionals, such as doctors, dentists and lawyers, may have their own profile when customers can contact them directly at the verified location during stated hours
One business, several servicesNot allowed: Google says not to create separate profiles for each service

If you made the copy yourself

Google says a duplicate created by mistake can be removed without affecting your verified listing, and that removal deletes all of its content and linked managers, so make sure you remove the right one. Its page on removing a profile adds that only primary owners can do it, that posts, photos and review replies by owners and managers are removed for good while customer reviews remain, and that the business might still show on Search and Maps afterwards.

As general advice, keep the listing that holds your verification, your reviews and your correct details.

If two listings for one business are on Maps

Google says to request a merge by reporting a suggestion on Google Maps, where a signed-in user can mark a place as a duplicate of another place. It says all merge requests are reviewed, that the profiles must represent the same business and should have the same information, and that a merge combines their reviews, though replies to reviews may be lost.

If the other listing is verified and owned by someone else, Google's page points to requesting ownership from its current owner; the related answer on what to do if someone else controls your Google Business Profile covers that, including when a former agency holds it.

If Google merged two real businesses

Google says that if profiles for two distinct, eligible businesses were merged by mistake, you can contact support to appeal the duplicate status. If both serve customers at the same location, it says you will be asked for evidence of permanent signage that clearly shows both.

Do not start a fresh profile

A new profile made to get around a problem can end up treated as the duplicate itself, as the related answer on why a business is not showing on Google Maps explains. After a move or a rename, the related answer on changing your business name or address on Google covers which profile to keep and when reviews move.
