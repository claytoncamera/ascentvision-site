Where should a small business start with AI?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

Start with one task that follows the same steps every time, write down how it is done today, and try a ready-made tool on it before paying anyone to build one. Keep a person on anything a customer will see, and compare the result with your notes after a month. Widen the use only once the first task holds.

The first month, in order

The order below is general advice, not an official standard.

  • Week one, pick one task. Choose something that follows the same steps each time and where a mistake is cheap to catch, such as appointment reminders (texts only with the person's consent) or a first draft of a common reply. The related answer on what a small business can automate with AI maps the kinds of task, and the related answer on telling if a task is worth automating scores one.
  • Write down how it is done today: the steps, who does them, how long they take, how often, and what goes wrong. Those notes are your before picture. Without them you cannot tell later whether anything improved.
  • Week two, try a bought tool before a built one. A ready-made tool on one task can show you what you actually need, and is usually easier to walk away from.
  • Weeks two and three, keep a person on anything a customer will see, and leave customer details out of the tool where you can.
  • Week four, measure and decide. Compare time, fixes and mistakes with your notes, then keep it, narrow it or stop. The related answer on how much time AI automation really saves shows how to count.
  • After that, widen one step at a time, and only after the first task has held for a while.

Free and low-cost tools

This section is general advice, not any agency's. Some AI tools have a free or lower-cost plan, and one can be enough to test a single task. Before you rely on one, have a second person look at what it produces, read what its plan does with the text you type, and decide who in the business may use it. The related answers on approving what AI sends to customers and on putting customer information into ChatGPT go further.

Where small firms stand

The Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey provides, in the words of its article, "a biweekly, nationally representative view" of AI implementation. For data collected from December 14, 2025 to May 3, 2026, it reports that less than 20% of firms with four or fewer employees reported using AI. The related answer on what a small business can automate with AI gives more of its figures.

This can be a reason for patience, not a deadline. A figure like this does not say which task suits your business.

One note on buying or building

Ascent Vision builds AI systems, so read the advice to try a bought tool first with that in mind. A built system can make sense later, once you know from the first task what you need. The related answer on the risks of using AI in a small business lists what to watch as you widen it.
