Does a small business need website analytics?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

Usually yes, kept simple. Website analytics shows how many people visit, which pages they read and where they came from, which helps you spot pages that are not working. It cannot tell you who became an inquiry or a customer; only your own lead records can. Use analytics as a supporting view, collect only what you need, and say in your privacy notice what you use.

What analytics can tell you

  • How many visits the site gets, and whether that is rising or falling.
  • Which pages people read, and which they leave quickly.
  • Where visitors came from, such as a search, a social post, another website or a typed-in address.
  • Whether visitors are mostly on phones or computers.
  • Often, roughly where in the country they are.

What only your lead records can tell you

Analytics counts visits, not people you can call back. It usually cannot tell you who sent an inquiry, whether they phoned instead, or whether they became a customer. Those answers come from recording every inquiry, where it came from and whether it was won, lost or still open. The related answer on lead attribution covers how that works.

That is why a visit count rising on its own is not proof that marketing is working. Read the two together: analytics shows where attention goes, and lead records show what it turned into.

Keep it simple

For most small businesses, a few numbers once a month is plenty: total visits, the most-read pages, the main sources of visitors and the pages people reach just before they contact you. If a page gets visits but never leads to an inquiry, that is often the first page worth improving. The related answer on telling whether a website change worked shows how to compare before and after.

Privacy: collect only what you need

Choose an analytics tool, and settings, that collect only what you will actually use. Most small businesses need counts and trends, not detailed records about individual visitors.

It is a good habit for your privacy notice to say which analytics tool you use and what it collects; the related answer on whether a website needs a privacy policy covers collecting only what you need.
