Is WordPress a good choice for a small business website?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

It can be, if someone will look after it. WordPress is free, open-source software. From WordPress.org you download it and install it on hosting you choose; WordPress.com is a hosted service run by Automattic on the same software, which handles hosting, updates and backups. Self-hosted WordPress gives the most control and asks the most upkeep, so the real question is who will do that work.

Two things called WordPress

WordPress.com's support guide sets out the difference this way (it is WordPress.com's own comparison):

WordPress.org (self-hosted)WordPress.com (hosted)
HostingYou find and pay for a hosting providerManaged hosting is built in
Updates, backups, securityDepend on your host and hosting packageHandled automatically
PluginsCustom plugins can be installed, though some could compromise the siteOn paid plans, with updates managed for you
HelpSupport forums or your hostWordPress.com support

What WordPress gives you

WordPress.org says the software is licensed under the GPL, version 2 or later, which gives everyone the freedom to run it for any purpose, study and change it, and share copies, including changed ones. WordPress.com's guide says any WordPress site can be moved to or from WordPress.com, and that with either version you own your own content.

In practice, many designers know WordPress, and a self-hosted site can usually move to a new host without a rebuild.

What it asks of you

WordPress.com's guide says the self-hosted route means installing WordPress on your host's server, then building and maintaining the site on your own, including the theme and plugins. WordPress's plugin documentation adds that plugins need updates and that a backup should come first. The related answer on what a CMS is covers plugins in plain words.

Who it usually suits

  • A business with a designer or provider who will keep it updated, in writing.
  • A site that needs many pages, regular articles or a feature a simpler builder lacks.
  • An owner who wants full control over hosting, code and add-ons.
  • Less suited: an owner who wants to edit occasionally and never think about updates, with nobody to apply them. A hosted builder is often the calmer choice there.

It is about upkeep more than Google

The platform usually matters less to being found than what the pages say. The related answer on whether a Wix or Squarespace site is bad for Google covers what Google's guide says. A neglected WordPress site with old plugins can be easier to break into, so whoever owns the updates matters more than the brand.
