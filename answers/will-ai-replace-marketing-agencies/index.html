Will AI replace marketing agencies, and do you still need one?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

No one can say for certain how far AI will change marketing work. AI tools can already help with drafts, summaries and first checks, while knowing the business, checking facts and answering for results usually still need a person. So the useful question for an owner is how an agency uses AI: which parts it hands to a tool, who checks the output, and what it can actually show you.

What to expect from an agency now

The related answers on whether AI can do your SEO, on hiring a marketing employee or an agency, and on what a small business can use AI for cover the tools, the hire and an owner's own use. This one covers what to expect from an agency now that those tools exist.

Inside the work: the tool's part and a person's part

The U.S. Small Business Administration's guide advises that a business using free AI tools or software have another person review all AI products, to help make sure they accurately represent the business.

For pages, posts, emails, review replies and messages, the related answer on what a small business can use AI for sets out what a tool can draft and what a person must decide; the one place an agency's AI changes what you receive is the report.

A warning about AI claims in marketing services

On August 27, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission finalized orders settling charges against a media and marketing company and two marketing firms it worked with. In three complaints, the FTC alleged they deceived customers by claiming an AI-powered service could target local ads using conversations captured from consumers' smart devices; the release adds that, contrary to the companies' claims, the service was not based on voice data. The orders prohibit each firm from misrepresenting the qualities or features of its advertising or marketing services. As general advice, when an agency says its AI does something, ask to see it work, and get it in writing. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

What to ask any agency about AI

  • Which parts of my work will AI do, and which will a person do?
  • Who checks AI drafts for facts, prices and claims before anything goes out in my name?
  • Will my customers' details go into an AI tool? See the related answer on putting customer information into ChatGPT.
  • Will anything AI-made be presented as a real review, customer or photo? It should not; see the related answer on using AI to write reviews.
  • How will I judge the work: inquiries by source, each followed to won, lost or still open?

So do you still need an agency?

As general advice, judge an employee, an agency or a tool the same way: by the inquiries you can trace to it, and by who answers for mistakes made in your name. An agency's written agreement can say who checks AI work; the related answer on what a marketing agency contract should include covers the other clauses.
