What should a marketing agency contract say before you sign it?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 25, 2026

Before signing, check that the contract says in plain words what work is included, how long it runs and whether it renews automatically, how and when either side can end it, that your accounts, content and data stay in your business's name, what reports you get, how your information is kept confidential, and what you receive back when it ends. If any of those is missing or vague, ask for it in writing first.

The clauses to look for

This is not a template, just what a fair agreement usually covers. The related answer on what an SEO proposal should include checks the offer; this list is for the agreement you sign.

When requests grow past the agreed work without a new agreement, that is often called scope creep. A scope clause that lists what is included and what is not, and a short written note agreed before any extra work starts, usually settles it.

ClauseWhat it should make clear
ScopeThe work included, what is not, and how extra work is agreed before it starts
Term and renewalAny minimum term, whether it renews automatically and the deadline to stop a renewal
Ending itThe notice either side must give, in what form, and what happens to work in progress
OwnershipAccounts, logins, content and finished work belong to the business; the agency is added as a user
ReportingHow often you hear, with inquiries by source and whether each was won, lost or still open
ApprovalsNothing is published in your name without your sign-off
Confidentiality and dataWho can see your customer and lead data, and that it comes back in a usable file at the end
Who does the workYour named contact, and whether any work goes to subcontractors

Red flags in the fine print

As general advice, slow down if you see any of these:

For a monthly-fee arrangement, the related answer on what a marketing retainer is lists the warning signs that belong to that model.

  • Automatic renewal or a long minimum term, with a short or hard-to-find window to cancel.
  • Your domain, ad accounts or business profiles in the agency's name; the related answers on who should own your domain name and who should own your ad accounts explain why that matters.
  • Content, phone numbers or the website itself licensed to you only while you keep paying.
  • A fee or a delay before you get your own accounts and data back.
  • A promised ranking, AI recommendation or number of leads, next to a vague scope.

Before you sign

Read it in full and ask for any change in writing. The related answer on questions to ask before hiring an SEO agency covers checking the agency, and the related answer on what to tell a marketing agency before it starts covers agreeing how the work will be judged. The FTC's guide to scams for small businesses suggests that, before doing business with a new company, you search its name online with the term "scam" or "complaint".

At Ascent Vision, clients own their domain, content and data, every lead carries its source, and there are no published prices: every engagement is scoped on a call.

If you want to leave a contract you signed

Secure your own access before giving notice; the related answer on how to switch marketing agencies gives the order and a handover checklist. Send notice the way the contract asks, keep a dated copy, and ask in writing what you will get back and when. Whether anything is owed for leaving early usually depends mostly on the contract's words; a lawyer can read a disputed one. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.
