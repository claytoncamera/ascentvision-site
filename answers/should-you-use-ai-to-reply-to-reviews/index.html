Should you use AI to write your replies to reviews?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

AI can help with a first draft, but a person who knows the job should edit and post every reply. AI was not there, can state wrong details with confidence and can repeat itself, and Google's own reply tips advise against sending everyone the same thank-you. Google now offers drafting itself: its help page says Gemini can draft review replies once an eligible owner connects the Business Profile.

Where an AI draft can help

  • A first draft for each review when several are waiting, so the time goes into checking facts and tone, not starting from nothing.
  • Finding a calm tone for an angry review, before you rewrite it in your own words.
  • Shortening a reply that runs long. Google's tips say to keep replies short and simple, because people might stop reading before the main point.
  • A second pair of eyes on a reply before it is public; the related answer on whether AI can write your website covers the same strengths and limits for pages.

What still needs a person

Google says an approved reply is posted publicly under the review as the business, and the reviewer is notified. As general advice, a person who knows the job should check every draft for these before it goes up:

  • Facts. The AI was not on the job; a draft can describe work you did not do or a problem that never happened. Keep only what is true.
  • Privacy. Google's tips say never to share a reviewer's private information, so cut any address, bill or personal detail a draft picks up.
  • Offers and promises. Google's tips say to avoid using replies to offer deals or promotions, and Google strictly prohibits offering incentives to change a review or remove a negative one. A promised refund in a draft is a public commitment.
  • Voice. Read the draft aloud: it should sound like your business, not a template. For negative reviews, Google's tips suggest signing off with a name or initials.

Why identical replies read badly

Google's tips say to make replies count: instead of sending everyone the same thank-you, focus on reviews where you can share a helpful update or answer a question. As general advice, a page of replies that all say the same thing can suggest nobody read the reviews, and a tool that answers every review automatically, with no person checking, can produce exactly that.

What Google itself offers

Google's help page on managing a Business Profile with the Gemini web app says that, once the profile is connected, Gemini can help with "drafting replies to reviews" and with summarizing customer feedback from reviews. To connect, Google says you must be an owner or manager of only one verified Business Profile, be 18 or over, use a personal Google Account rather than a work or school one, and have Keep Activity on; it is available only in the Gemini web app.

As general advice, read a Gemini draft as carefully as one from any other tool. A third-party reply tool that posts for you usually needs access to your profile, and the related answer on giving someone access to your Google Business Profile covers which role to give. For what a good reply says, see the related answer on how to respond to a negative Google review; writing reviews themselves, as opposed to replies, is a separate line covered in the related answer on whether AI can write your website.
