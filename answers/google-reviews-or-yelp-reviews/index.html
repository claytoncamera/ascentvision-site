Which matter more for a service business: Google reviews or Yelp reviews?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

For most service businesses, Google usually deserves the review request, because Google lets you ask customers for reviews and shows them next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search. Yelp says businesses should not ask anyone for reviews, and its star rating counts only the reviews its software recommends. So ask every customer for a Google review, keep your Yelp page claimed and answered, and let Yelp reviews arrive on their own.

Why the same business can show two ratings

Google's page on review scores says the score is the average of all ratings published on Google for the business, and that a new review can take up to 2 weeks to show in it. Yelp says reviews its software does not recommend stay readable through a link at the bottom of the page but do not count toward the star rating or review count. How that software decides is covered in the related answer on why Yelp hides some reviews.

The two sites also collect reviews differently. On Google, many reviews may come from customers the business asked; Yelp says it wants reviews from people inspired to write on their own. As general advice, a gap between your two ratings is common and is usually not a sign that either site is wrong.

Where to send customers

  • Ask for Google reviews. Google says the review link and QR code are under Read reviews, then Get more reviews, on your profile, and that the QR code can only be made in a computer browser. Customers need a Google Account to post. The related answer on getting more reviews the right way covers how and when to ask.
  • Leave Yelp out of the request, even as a second link. Yelp's rule is not to ask anyone, so a message offering both sites is still a request for a Yelp review.
  • Keep the Yelp page active without asking. Yelp's own suggestions include responding to reviews and putting a "Find us on Yelp" sticker on a storefront or service vehicle.

Which one your customers read

Your own customers are the better guide. As general advice, ask every new customer how they found you, and note when they mention reading reviews on one site or the other; the related answer on "how did you hear about us" options covers the wording. If Yelp keeps coming up, its page deserves regular replies; if no one mentions it, Google usually deserves most of the effort. Why reviews matter in the first place is covered in the related answer on whether online reviews matter.

In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey (1,002 US adults, February 2026), 45% said they had written a Google review in the past year, 34% a Facebook review and 24% a Yelp review. For reading reviews, the same survey put Google first at 71%, with ChatGPT and other AI tools used by 45%. As general advice, ask your own last ten customers which sites they checked; that beats any national figure.
