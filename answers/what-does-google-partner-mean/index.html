What does it mean when an agency says it is a Google Partner?

Ascent Vision · Published October 21, 2026

Google gives the Partner badge to agencies and other companies that manage Google Ads accounts for other businesses and meet its requirements. Those requirements concern Google Ads: an optimization score, ad spend and Google Ads certifications. So when an agency says it is a Google Partner, that speaks to paid-ads skills and activity. By itself it does not show that the agency can improve free search rankings, map listings or AI answers.

What Google says a Partner is

Google describes its Partners program as designed for advertising agencies and other third parties that manage Google Ads accounts on behalf of other businesses. It has three levels: Member, Partner and Premier Partner. A company joins as a Member by signing up with its Google Ads manager account, and becomes a Partner by also meeting Google’s Partner requirements.

Google says the Partner badge “shows that you have the latest Google Ads expertise”, and that a Partner can show the badge on its website and marketing materials. Premier Partner is a further step: Google reserves it for the top 3% of participating companies in a country each year, judged against how other companies perform.

What an agency has to meet for the badge

Google lists three requirements and says it checks them daily. All three apply to the agency’s registered Google Ads manager account:

  • Performance: a minimum optimization score of 70%.
  • Spend: a set minimum of ad spend over 90 days, counted across the accounts the agency manages.
  • Certifications: at least half of its account strategists certified in Google Ads, with further conditions by product area.

What the badge does not tell you

All three requirements are about Google Ads, and they are counted for the agency as a whole, not for your business. On its own, the badge does not tell you how well any one client’s campaigns did. As Google lists them, all three are Google Ads measures; none is about free search rankings, free map listings or AI answers.

The related answer on whether Google Ads help your free rankings covers how ads and free results relate. An agency can be an able Google Ads manager and still have little experience in free search or AI answers, or the reverse. Ask about the service you actually want.

How to check the claim

  • Ask what the badge reflects in practice, and which service you are buying: paid ads, free search or AI answers.
  • Look for the agency in Google’s Partners directory. Google says companies with Partner or Premier Partner status can be listed there, with the product areas in which they hold Google Ads certifications. Companies may opt out of the listing, so a missing entry is a reason to ask, not proof of a false claim.
  • Partner status is a real Google Ads program, but treat any claim that it brings better free rankings as a warning sign; the related answer on how to spot an SEO scam covers it.
  • Put the same questions to a Partner as to any agency; the related answer on questions to ask before hiring an SEO agency lists them.
