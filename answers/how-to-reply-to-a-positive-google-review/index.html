How should a business reply to a positive Google review?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Keep it short and specific. Thank the customer, mention one real detail of the job, and stop there. Google's reply tips say to be conversational, not promotional: skip deals and promotions, and share "a detail that reinforces their experience and encourages a return visit". Write each reply fresh rather than pasting one thank-you everywhere, leave out private details, and never offer anything in return for a review.

A simple shape for the reply

Google's reply tips say to keep replies short and simple, because people might stop reading before they reach the main point. Two tips Google lists for negative reviews, addressing the reviewer by name and signing off with your name or initials, usually work for good ones too. As general advice, a reply in four parts:

  • Thank them, using the name shown on the review.
  • Name one true detail: the job, the area or the person who did the work. Google says a customer reviewing a service business may be asked to choose the service provided, and that the choice shows in a written review, which can give you that detail.
  • Answer anything they asked, or add a helpful update.
  • Sign off with your name or initials, and stop.

What to leave out

Google's tips say your reviewers are already customers, so avoid using a reply to offer deals or promotions. As general advice, also leave out anything private, such as the customer's address, what they paid or details of their home, even in a friendly reply; Google's privacy tip sits with its advice on negative reviews, but a reply is public either way. Do not ask the customer to edit the review or add to it.

Never offer anything in return for the review. Google says offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, for reviews is strictly prohibited; the related answer on giving customers a discount for a review explains the rules.

Why copied replies read badly

Google's tips say that instead of sending the same thank-you to everyone, focus on reviews where you can share a helpful update or answer a question. The related answer on whether to use AI to reply to reviews covers why a run of identical replies looks unread, and what to check in any drafted reply. Whether customers care about replies is covered by the survey in the related answer on whether online reviews matter.

Timing, old reviews and later changes

Google lists a prompt response among its tips for negative reviews, saying it shows you value feedback; as general advice, replying within a few days usually suits good reviews too. An older review with no reply can still be answered. Google says the reviewer is notified when you reply, so a short, warm note reaches them even months later, and you can edit or delete your reply afterwards from your Business Profile.

Google also lets you copy a direct link to a customer's review to share it. Using a customer's words in your own marketing has its own rules, covered in the related answer on putting customer reviews on your website. For an unhappy reviewer, the related answer on responding to a negative Google review takes over.
