How do you get control of your website back when the designer has disappeared?

Ascent Vision · Published October 18, 2026

Start with the domain, because everything else can be rebuilt behind it. Check who is recorded as its holder: if that is your business, recover the registrar account and move the domain to an account you control; if it is the designer, you need their cooperation, the registrar's help, or in the worst case a new domain. Then recover the hosting and platform accounts with proof of identity and billing, and save every page that is still visible before anything disappears.

First, find out what you control

A website is really four things: the domain, the hosting, the account on the website platform, and the content itself. You may be locked out of one and not the others. The related answer on who should own your domain name shows how to check who is recorded as the domain's holder, which decides how hard the rest will be.

Recovering the domain

Domains are governed by ICANN's rules for registrars. Under its Transfer Policy, the registered name holder controls whether a domain moves between registrars, and a registrar may refuse a transfer only for listed reasons. So if your business is the named holder, you can recover access through the registrar's own account-recovery process with business and billing records, then move the domain to an account only you control. One timing note: after a change of registrant, the policy has the registrar lock the domain against moving to another registrar for 60 days, unless the registrar offers an opt-out before the change, so allow for that. This is a plain summary of ICANN's policy, not legal advice.

If the designer registered the domain in their own name, the registrar's record treats them as the holder, however unfair that feels. Ask them in writing to transfer it; many will. If they are unreachable, ask the registrar what it would need, and consider having a lawyer send the request if the domain matters enough. ICANN's registrant guidance also expects holders to provide accurate contact information and keep it current, which is one reason recovery goes better when the record was right from the start.

Hosting and platform accounts

Hosting companies and website platforms usually have an account-recovery path for a verified owner; expect to show ID, business registration and billing records. If the account was the designer's own and your site merely lives inside it, support can often explain your options, but you may not get that account itself. Either way, open fresh accounts in your business's name before pointing the domain anywhere new.

If you cannot get back in

Save what is still visible while the site is up: copy the text of every page, download the images you supplied, and keep screenshots of the rest. A rebuild on a domain and accounts you own is often faster than a long fight over the old ones.

Prevention is covered by the related answers on how to switch marketing agencies and what a website design contract should include: accounts in your name from day one, and ownership in writing.

Sources

  1. ICANN, "Transfer Policy" (effective 2016)
  2. ICANN, "Registrants' Benefits and Responsibilities"
