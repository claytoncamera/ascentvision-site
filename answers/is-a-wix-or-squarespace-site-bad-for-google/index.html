Is a Wix or Squarespace website bad for showing up on Google?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Usually not. Google's starter guide names no website builder and says that, for Google to find a site, you usually don't need to do anything except publish it. Wix says its sites are designed to be search engine friendly on any plan, and Squarespace says the content you add and how you present it play a big role in how well people find you. The limits owners tend to hit are control, speed and moving out.

What Google's guide says

Google's SEO Starter Guide names no website builder. It says most sites in Google's results are found and added automatically, that you usually don't need to do anything except publish your site, and that with a content management system you might not need to do anything technical to your page titles beyond writing good ones. It is also plain that there are "no secrets here that'll automatically rank your site first in Google".

As general advice, results usually depend on what a site says, how fast it loads on a phone and how useful it is, which the common builders can usually handle.

What Wix and Squarespace say about their own sites

Wix's help article on SEO says Wix sites are "designed to be search engine friendly", whether or not they are upgraded to a Premium plan. It adds that third-party SEO tools can sometimes struggle to read a Wix page fully, which may lead to inaccurate reports.

Squarespace's SEO checklist says its sites are built so that search engines can read them cleanly, but that the content you add and how you present it play a big role in how well people can find you. It recommends a custom domain over the built-in .squarespace.com address, saying this makes it easier for customers to find you in search results.

The limits owners actually run into

As general advice:

  • Control: you can usually change only what the builder allows, so a missing feature may mean an add-on or a wait.
  • Speed: a site on any builder can turn slow on a phone once its pages carry large images, video and many add-ons; test your own pages on a phone.
  • Moving out: the related answer on whether you can move a Wix or Squarespace website covers what each company says can leave.
  • Ownership: keep the builder account and the domain in the business's name, as the related answer on how to choose a website platform explains.

When a rebuild is pitched on this fear

A seller who says your site must leave Wix or Squarespace before Google will show it is going further than Google's guide and both companies' own help pages. As general advice, ask what specific problem the rebuild fixes, how it will be measured in inquiries and what happens to your domain. A scary automated report is not proof on its own, given what Wix says about third-party tools.

If the site works but is unclear or slow, improving it where it is usually comes first; the related answers on whether to upgrade or rebuild your website and on whether a website template is good enough explain how to tell. If you do switch, the related answer on whether a new website or domain will hurt your Google results covers what to keep.
