Where should a small business start with marketing?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

Start with the basics, in order. Know what a customer is worth to you. Make sure every inquiry reaches a person and is recorded with its source. Fix the website basics so visitors can see what you do and how to contact you. Then work on getting found through search, AI answers, referrals and reviews. Finally, check the numbers once a month and adjust.

The order, in one table

StepWhy it comes hereHow you know it is done
1. Know what a customer is worthIt tells you how much time and money a new customer justifies.You can name the typical value of a job and how often customers come back.
2. Make sure inquiries reach you and are trackedMore visibility is wasted if calls go to voicemail or forms go nowhere.Every call and form reaches a person and is recorded with where it came from.
3. Fix the website basicsVisitors who cannot tell what you do, or how to reach you, leave.The homepage says what you do, where, and how to get in touch, and it works on a phone.
4. Get foundNow new attention has somewhere useful to go.More inquiries arrive from search, AI answers, referrals and reviews.
5. Measure monthlyIt shows what is working, so you can do more of it.You review the same few numbers on the same day each month.

Why the order matters

Many owners start with step four, because getting found feels like marketing. But extra visitors are worth little if the phone goes unanswered or the website does not say what you do. The first three steps usually cost less, and they make everything after them work better.

What the SBA suggests

The U.S. Small Business Administration's marketing guide lays out a marketing plan with sections that include a target market ("Describe your audience in detail") and a closing section, "Measure and update your plan". That section asks you to "compare your marketing and sales costs to the revenue it generates". Step five above is the monthly version of that comparison.

Ways to get found

Search, AI answers, referrals and reviews are the usual routes, and none of them needs an ad budget. The related answer on getting leads without ads covers each one. No one can promise that an assistant will recommend a particular business.

What to check each month

  • How many inquiries arrived, and from which sources.
  • How many became paying jobs: won, lost or still open.
  • What those jobs were worth.
  • One change to make before next month.

Sources

  1. U.S. Small Business Administration, Manage your business: marketing and sales
