What is a sales pipeline for a small service business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

A sales pipeline is the set of stages an inquiry moves through after it has been answered: a lead system gets the inquiry in and answered, and the pipeline tracks what happens next. For a small service business, a few plain stages usually cover it: new, contacted, quote or visit booked, quote sent, and an outcome of won, lost or still open. Knowing which stage each lead is in shows where jobs stall, so you can fix that step instead of only chasing more inquiries.

The stages

StageWhat it meansWhat moves it on
NewAn inquiry has arrived and no one has replied yet.A first reply.
ContactedYou have replied by email, or by call or text only with the person's consent.Agreeing a visit, a call, or the details you need to price the job.
Quote or visit bookedA time is set to see the job or talk it through.The visit or call happens and you can price the work.
Quote sentThey have your price and scope in writing.Their yes or no, often after a follow-up or two.
OutcomeWon or lost, with the reason if lost. Anything not yet decided is still open.Nothing; review outcomes once a month.

Why stages help

A pipeline shows where work stalls. Many leads stuck at new usually point to slow replies. Many stuck at quote sent can point to the quote itself, or to follow-up that stops too soon. Many lost after a visit can mean a mismatch on price or fit that a few questions earlier might have caught.

It also shows what is coming. The number of quotes out gives a rough sense of how busy the next few weeks may be.

Keep it simple

  • Use the stages above, or rename them for your trade, but make each one a step that has clearly happened, not a feeling.
  • Keep every lead in exactly one stage at a time.
  • Move a lead on the day something happens.
  • Close a lead as lost when it has gone quiet after your last follow-up, rather than leaving it open forever.

Where it lives

A pipeline does not need special software. A stage column in a shared sheet is enough at first, and the related answer on setting up a simple lead tracker covers the other columns.

A short weekly look at every open lead, oldest first, is usually enough to make sure nothing is forgotten.
