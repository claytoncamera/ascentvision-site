What is a Google Business Profile, and does a service business need one?

Ascent Vision · Published October 19, 2026

A Google Business Profile is the free listing Google shows for a business on Search and Maps: name, hours, contact details, photos, reviews and a short description. Google says creating one is free. A business that customers visit, or that travels to customers, can have one, and for most local service businesses it is worth claiming: it is often the first thing a customer sees, and the owner controls part of it but not all of it.

What the profile is

A Business Profile is the panel Google shows for a business on Search and Maps: name, hours, phone, address or service area, photos, reviews, a short description and a website link. Google's own page says: "Yes, creating a Business Profile and listing your business on Google is free". It is not a website; it is the summary a customer sees before deciding whether to visit the site or call.

A profile can exist before the owner claims it, because Google builds profiles from several sources; claiming it gives the owner a say in what it shows.

What the owner controls

Once claimed, the owner can edit hours and contact details, add photos, write the description, publish posts, and reply to reviews. Google's guidelines ask for regular customer-facing hours and a description that gives useful information about what the business offers.

A business that travels to customers sets service areas instead of showing a storefront. Google's help page says an owner can set up to 20 service areas by city, postal code or other area, and should remove the address from the profile if customers are not served there.

What customers and Google add

The owner does not control the whole profile. Google says profile information comes from publicly available sources such as the business's own website, licensed data from third parties, users who contribute facts and content such as photos and reviews, and Google's own interactions with the business. Google says users can suggest an edit or flag a profile for removal.

So customers add reviews and photos, the public can propose changes, and Google decides some things itself: its guidelines say it can detect category information from the website and from mentions across the web, and that it determines how best to display the address. The owner's job is to keep the parts it controls accurate and to answer what others add.

Does a service business need one?

Usually, yes. If customers in your area search for what you do, the profile is often the first thing they see, and it costs only the time to claim and maintain it. A business that sells online or serves clients anywhere by video call gets less from it; the related answer on what local SEO is explains when place matters.

Two cautions. A profile does not promise a position: Google decides which profiles to show. And customers can post reviews whether or not you claim the profile, so an unclaimed profile still shapes what people read about you, with no one answering; the related answer on whether online reviews matter shows how much that weighs.
