Should you buy off-the-shelf software or have something built?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

Try off-the-shelf software first if it can do most of the job, because it is usually quicker to start and the vendor keeps it working. Consider having software built when your own process is what sets you apart, when you are bending your work around a tool, or when no product connects the pieces you rely on. A bought product can also show you what you actually need.

The honest case for buying first

Ready-made software has usually been used by many other businesses, so many of its problems have been found and fixed. The vendor updates it and answers questions. You can often start within days, and you can leave if it does not suit you.

A custom build needs someone to maintain it for as long as you use it. Many owners find that a bought product with the right settings covers nearly everything. The related answer on whether field service software is worth it shows how to judge one product category.

A decision test in owner terms

Read these in order. If the first answer is yes, buy. Yes to several of the others points toward having something built, or toward a bought product with a custom piece added.

  • Can a product do the main job with settings alone? If yes, buy it.
  • Are you changing how you work to suit the tool, and is it hurting you? If yes, a build may be worth discussing.
  • Does the way you do this job set you apart in a way customers notice? If yes, a build may protect it.
  • Does it have to connect things that products do not connect? If yes, a small build or an integration may be needed.
  • Will you rely on it daily for years? A tool you depend on is worth owning or understanding properly.

A middle route, and what to settle first

Often the better answer is a bought product connected to your other tools, with a small custom piece only where the product falls short. Whichever route you choose, settle who owns the result and the accounts before work starts.
