What is a digital agency, and how is it different from a marketing agency?

Ascent Vision · Published October 31, 2026

A digital agency is an outside company that sells online services, usually some mix of websites, search visibility, ads, social media and email. A marketing agency usually sells similar work with more focus on bringing in customers. The two names overlap and mean different things to different companies, so the label tells you little. Compare what each will deliver in writing, who owns the accounts, how results are reported and how you can leave.

Why the names tell you little

No rulebook defines these labels. Companies usually pick the name that sounds right to buyers, so two agencies with the same label can sell very different things, and two with different labels can sell nearly the same thing. A web agency may run ads, a marketing agency may build websites and a digital agency may do both.

A name can hint at where a company started, such as design, advertising or websites. It does not tell you what you will receive, who will do the work or what you will see afterward.

The labels in one table

LabelWhat it usually sells (a rough guide)
Digital agencyOnline work chosen from a menu: websites, ads, search, social media, email
Full-service digital agencySeveral of those under one contract and one report
Marketing agencyWork meant to bring in customers: messaging, ads, content, search, sometimes the website
Creative agencyBrand, design, video and copywriting
Web agencyWebsites and their upkeep, sometimes hosting
Growth agencyAds, tests and changes to a page or an offer, aimed at measurable growth

What to compare instead

  • Who does the work: the people named to you, or a subcontractor you never meet.
  • Whether the agreement renews on its own, and what notice it takes to leave.
  • The rest of the list is the same for any label: the deliverables in writing, the accounts in the business's name, a report that starts with inquiries and customers by source.
