How do you say no to a customer politely?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Thank them, give a plain no with a short, true reason, and offer what you can do instead. Keep it calm and quick, and put it in writing when it changes the agreed work. A clear no usually keeps more goodwill than a vague maybe that ends in a missed date or a surprise bill. Never invent a policy to hide behind; say the real reason in a sentence.

A pattern that works for most requests

  • Thank them for asking.
  • Say no plainly, with one true reason.
  • Offer what you can do instead, if there is something.
  • Say what happens next.

Sample lines

RequestWhat you might say
A discount"Thanks for asking. I can't lower the price for the same job, because that is what it takes to do it properly. If it helps, we could do the front yard now and the back in the spring."
Extra work outside the quote"Happy to look at that. It isn't part of what we agreed, so I'll send a short written change with the extra time and cost before we start."
A date you cannot make"I can't make Saturday without rushing your job. The earliest I can do it properly is Tuesday morning. Would that work?"

Why a clear no is kinder

A vague maybe often feels polite in the moment, but it can turn into a missed date, a rushed job or a bill the customer did not expect. A short, honest no lets them plan, and many people respect it. Turning down extra requests can also protect the work you already agreed to do well.

Put changes in writing

When the request changes the agreed work, send a short written note of what changes and how it affects time or cost, and wait for a yes before you start. If a customer asks for money back after something went wrong, treat it as a complaint first; the related answer on responding to a customer complaint covers making one clear offer you can keep.

What not to do

  • Invent a policy or blame someone else.
  • Say yes to avoid an awkward moment and then miss it.
  • Apologize over and over, which can sound like the answer might change.
  • Argue in public. Take it to a call or a private message.
  • Dodge a price question; the related answer on leads who ask for the price first covers that one.
  • Leave someone with nowhere to go when you do not do the job; the related answer on leads you cannot serve has a short example of pointing them elsewhere.
