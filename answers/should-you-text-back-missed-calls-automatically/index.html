Should you send an automatic text when you miss a call?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

It can help, but send an automatic text only with the person's consent. Keep it to the missed call, with no offers: say you missed the call and how they will get an answer. Log every missed call as a lead whether or not you text. The FCC's consumer guide says its consent rules cover texts sent with an autodialer. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

When an automatic text can help

A caller who reaches voicemail may try the next business on the list, so a quick acknowledgment can keep the conversation open, for example outside working hours or while you are on a job. It fits only when the text goes to someone who has agreed to get texts from you, such as a customer who asked you to text. For anyone else, a clear voicemail greeting and a fast callback can do much of the same work.

Whether a missed call alone counts as consent is a legal question this answer does not settle, and the safe habit is to ask first. The related answer on texting a new lead or a past customer covers consent and opting out.

What the FCC's guide says

The FCC's consumer guide on robocalls and texts says its rules ban text messages sent to a mobile phone using an autodialer unless the phone owner previously gave consent, or the message is sent for emergency purposes. It says commercial texts require written consent, while for informational texts the consent may be oral. It also says a person may opt out of any robotext "at any time and in any reasonable manner" even after giving consent.

An automatic text is sent by software, not typed. Whether a given text-back system counts as an autodialer, and whether a particular text counts as informational, are legal questions this answer does not settle. The guide was last reviewed in February 2026; the related answer on texting a new lead covers how consent is taken back, including the FCC's October 2026 order once it takes effect. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

What the text should say

Keep it to who you are, that you missed the call, that the message is automatic and when a person will reply, plus a plain way to stop further texts; treat any clear request to stop, in any words, as final. Keep it about their call, not an ad. The related answer on after-hours inquiries covers what an automatic reply should say.

Log every missed call as a lead

Treat a missed call as an inquiry until you know otherwise, even with no voicemail, and add it to your lead list the same day; the related answer on tracking phone call leads covers what to record.

Also note whether a text went out and whether the person replied, so after a month you can see whether texting helped. The related answer on tracking phone call leads covers what to record, and the related answer on answering services covers answering the call itself.
