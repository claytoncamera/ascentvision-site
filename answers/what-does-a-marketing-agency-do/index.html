What does a marketing agency actually do for a small business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

A marketing agency does outside work meant to bring a business more customers: its website, being found on Google and in AI answers, ads, social media, the handling of new inquiries, or several of these at once. What matters to an owner is what it hands over: a plan in plain words, a dated record of the work, accounts kept in the business's name, and a monthly count of inquiries by source. Choose the kind by the problem you have.

The common kinds, and what each should hand you

Kind of agencyWhat you should be able to see
WebsiteOwner logins to the site and hosting, and a list of every page and change
Search and AI answers (SEO, GEO)Inquiries from search and from AI answers, each tagged as its own channel, and a dated change list
Paid adsThe ad account in your name, what was spent and the inquiries the ads brought in
Social mediaWhat was posted, and the messages and inquiries that came from it
Lead handlingEvery inquiry recorded with its source and followed to won, lost or still open
Full-serviceAll of the above in one report, with one person accountable

Choose by the problem, not the label

Too few people getting in touch usually points to search, AI answers, the website or ads; inquiries that arrive and then go cold usually point to lead handling, and more visibility rarely fixes that. The related answer on who can help improve your online presence has three quick questions that show which problem you have.

What any agency should hand over

  • A plan in plain words: what will be done this month, and what will be measured.
  • A dated record of what was changed or published in your name.
  • Every account held in the business's name, with the agency added as a user.
  • A monthly report that starts with inquiries and customers by source, not traffic.
  • A straight answer to what happens to your accounts and records if you stop.
