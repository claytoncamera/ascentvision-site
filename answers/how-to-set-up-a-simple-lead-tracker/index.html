How do you set up a simple lead tracker?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

Use one shared sheet or board with a row for every inquiry and eight columns: date, name, contact details, what they need, source channel, first reply time, outcome and notes. Follow one rule: every inquiry from every channel goes in the same day it arrives. Then review it once a month to see where work comes from, how fast you answer and which inquiries turn into paid jobs.

Pick one place

Use one shared sheet or board that everyone who answers inquiries can open, including on a phone. The related answer on CRMs covers when a simple sheet is enough and when to move on.

The columns

ColumnWhat goes in it
DateThe day the inquiry arrived.
NameThe person, and the company if there is one.
ContactPhone and email, as they gave them.
What they needThe request, in their own words.
Source channelOne of: search, AI answer, social, referral, paid, email, campaign, direct. A returning customer counts as direct.
First reply timeHow long it took from the inquiry to your first reply.
OutcomeWon, lost or still open.
NotesQuotes sent, follow-up dates and, if lost, why.

The one rule: every inquiry, same day

Every call, form, email, message and walk-in goes in on the day it arrives, including missed calls and inquiries that look like a poor fit. Skipped rows are what make trackers lie.

Fill the source channel by asking how they heard about you, and follow up by call or text only with the person's consent. Keep the sheet private and shared only with the people who answer inquiries.

The monthly review

  • How many inquiries came from each source channel.
  • How many of each were won, lost or still open.
  • Your typical first reply time, and the slowest replies.
  • Leads still open after a few weeks: follow up once more or close them as lost.
  • The most common reasons in the notes for lost work.
