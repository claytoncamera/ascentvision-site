How do you market a mobile or home-based service business with no storefront?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Show where you go instead of where you are. On Google, a business that travels to customers is a service-area business: Google's guidelines say it should hide its address and keep one profile with a designated service area. Then let the website name the towns you cover and explain how booking works, make your vehicle and job sites easy to recognize, ask every customer for an honest review and for referrals, and make sure calls get answered while you are on a job.

What Google allows without a storefront

Google's guidelines say a service-area business, one that serves customers at their locations, should hide its address from customers and keep one profile with a designated service area. They also say a rented mailing address where the business does not operate, which Google calls a virtual office, is not eligible for a profile. The related answer on what a Google Business Profile is covers setting service areas.

Let the website do the storefront's job

As general advice: with no shop to walk into, the website is often where a customer decides whether you are real. Name the towns you serve, say plainly that you come to the customer, explain how booking works (by phone, by text with the customer's consent, or by form), and show your hours for answering. A photo of you, your vehicle or your work usually does more than a stock image.

Your vehicle and your jobs are the sign

A clean, lettered vehicle parked outside a job, a yard sign placed with the customer's permission, or a note left for the neighbors can do the work a storefront sign does. Give each one its own phone number or short web address so you can tell what it brought in; the related answer on tracking leads from flyers, signs and vehicles shows how, and these count as the campaign channel in your lead records.

Reviews, referrals and calls you miss on a job

  • Ask every customer for an honest review, with no reward attached.
  • Ask neighbors of a finished job, and businesses that serve the same customers without competing, to pass your name on. Record each one as a referral.
  • Expect to miss calls while your hands are busy. A short greeting with a callback time you can keep, and a routine for working the missed calls, matter more for a mobile business than for a shop; the related answer on handling leads after hours covers the routine.
