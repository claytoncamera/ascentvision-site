What does Google mean by 'helpful, people-first' content?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Google means content made mainly to help people rather than to rank. Its guide, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content", gives owners questions to ask of their own pages: would your audience find it useful if they came to you directly, does it show first-hand expertise, will a reader leave knowing enough to act, and is it clear who wrote it? For a service business, that usually means pages written for customers by people who know the work.

The short version

Google says its automated ranking systems are designed to prioritize helpful, reliable information created to benefit people, and defines people-first content as "content that's created primarily for people", not to manipulate search engine rankings. The related answer on why competitors show up on Google instead of you covers the guide's question on whether a page offers substantial value next to other results.

Google's people-first questions, in owner terms

Google says answering yes to its people-first questions means you are probably on the right track. Among them: would your existing or intended audience find the content useful if they came directly to you; does it show first-hand expertise, such as from having actually used a product or service; does the site have a primary purpose or focus; and will a reader leave feeling they learned enough to reach their goal?

As general advice, for a service business that usually means plain pages about the work you do, where you do it and what a customer can expect, written or checked by someone who does it.

Warning signs Google lists

Google also lists questions that, answered yes, are a warning sign to rethink how content is made. They include:

  • Is the content mainly made to attract visits from search engines?
  • Are you producing lots of content on many topics in the hope some of it performs?
  • Are you mainly summarizing what others say without adding much value?
  • Are you writing to a set word count? Google says it has no preferred word count.
  • Are you changing page dates to seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?

What E-E-A-T stands for

E-E-A-T is Google's shorthand for experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness. Google says its systems look for a mix of factors that show these aspects, and that "Of these aspects, trust is most important". It also says "E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor", that its systems give even more weight to it on topics that could significantly affect people's health, financial stability or safety, and that the search quality raters who check results against these ideas have no control over how pages rank.

Who, how and why

Google suggests asking who created a page, how and why. It strongly encourages accurate authorship information, such as bylines where readers would expect them, and calls why perhaps the most important question: the answer should be that the content exists primarily to help people. As general advice, a page that names the owner or crew who did the work, and shows real jobs, usually earns trust from customers too.
