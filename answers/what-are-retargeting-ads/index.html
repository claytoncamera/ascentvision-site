What are retargeting ads, and are they worth it for a service business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Retargeting, also called remarketing, shows your ads to people who have already interacted with your business, such as by visiting your website, while they browse or search elsewhere. Google now calls these lists "your data" segments, and Meta builds them as custom audiences. For a service business it tends to suit jobs people compare for days, less so emergencies. Judge it by the inquiries it brings, not by how often the ads are seen.

How Google and Meta describe it

Google's Ads Help says you can use your data to re-engage people who have previously interacted with your business, with ads shown as they browse Google or partner websites. The ways it lists include showing ads to past website visitors, showing ads to past visitors as they do follow-up searches on Google, reaching people who interacted with your YouTube videos or channel, and Customer Match, which uses contact information your customers have given you. It notes that the term remarketing has been replaced by "your data".

Meta's help page says a custom audience lets advertisers build audiences from their own data sources or Meta engagement data, including for retargeting. Its types include people who visited your website, a customer list of emails or phone numbers, people who interacted with your Facebook Page or Instagram business account, and people who opened or submitted a lead form on your ads.

When it can suit a service business

  • Jobs people research and compare before they book, such as a remodel or a large repair that can wait.
  • Seasonal work, where a reminder before the season can bring someone back.
  • It usually helps less with urgent jobs: someone with a burst pipe tends to call the first business that answers, before any second ad reaches them.
  • It needs enough visitors to work with. If few people visit your site, the list stays small, and Google's policy does not allow using these lists to target an overly narrow audience.

Rules the platforms set

Google's personalized advertising policy lists sensitive interest categories, health among them, and says advertisers promoting products or services in those categories cannot use advertiser-curated audiences, which include your data segments and Customer Match. Meta says custom audiences must not include information its terms do not allow, giving references to specific health conditions and financial status as examples, and that such audiences will be flagged and cannot be used.

Google also requires a site that uses your data segments to say in its privacy policy how it uses that data to advertise online, that third-party vendors including Google show its ads across the internet using cookies or device identifiers based on past visits, and how visitors can opt out. The related answer on whether a website needs a privacy policy covers the notice itself. This is a plain summary of Google's and Meta's policies, not legal advice.

Judge it by inquiries

Retargeting reaches people who already know your name, so some of them would have come back anyway. As general advice, log each lead from these ads as paid, with the campaign's name, follow it to won, lost or still open, and compare inquiries and jobs before and during the campaign rather than counting views.
