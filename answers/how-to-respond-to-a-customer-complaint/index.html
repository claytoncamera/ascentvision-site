How should a service business respond to a customer complaint?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Reply the same day if you can, and listen to the whole complaint before offering a fix. Then make one clear offer you can keep, say when it will happen, and confirm it in writing. Do what you said, check back, and log the complaint and how it ended, because patterns show what to fix. Handled early and privately, a complaint often never turns into a public review.

Answer fast, and listen first

A complaint usually gets harder to settle the longer it waits. Acknowledge it within hours, even if the fix will take longer: say you have it, who is looking at it and when you will be back in touch.

Reply in the channel the customer used, or offer a call and call only with the person's consent. Let them finish before you explain anything. Repeat back what you heard, ask what a good outcome would look like to them, and leave the question of whose fault it was for later.

Make one clear offer

  • Choose a fix you can actually deliver: come back and redo the work, put right the specific problem, or settle the bill fairly.
  • Make one offer, not a menu that keeps growing as the conversation goes on.
  • Say when it will happen and who will do it.
  • If you cannot do what they ask, say plainly what you can do instead.
  • Never tie the fix to a review, or to changing or removing one.

Put it in writing

After a call, send a short message that sums up what you heard, what you will do, by when, and who to contact. Keep it factual and calm, with no blame. It can protect both sides if memories differ later, and it shows the customer the promise was real.

Keep photos, dates and notes from the job with the complaint. If the customer stays unhappy after a fair offer, a polite final message that restates the offer in writing usually serves you better than more back and forth.

Log it, and look for patterns

Record each complaint next to the job: the date, what went wrong, what you offered and how it ended. Check back a few days after the fix to make sure it held. Once a month, read the log for repeats, such as the same step, the same service or the same handover, because a repeat points to a process to change, not a person to blame.
