What is GoHighLevel, and does a small business need it?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

GoHighLevel, which its maker calls HighLevel, is software that its home page describes as an AI-powered business operating system: a CRM plus forms, calendars, texting, call tracking, review requests and automations in one place. Agencies often run each client in a sub-account under the agency's own account. A small business does not need it by name. What it needs is to own its customer records, be able to export them, and know what happens if the agency relationship ends.

What HighLevel says it is

HighLevel's home page calls the product an AI-powered business operating system, with tools to "capture, nurture and close new leads into bookings, sales, reviews and repeat customers". The tools it names include, among many others, the ones listed here.

  • A CRM, sales pipelines and lead scoring.
  • Forms, surveys, websites, funnels and landing pages.
  • Call tracking, missed call text-back and Voice AI.
  • One conversation stream for texts, Messenger, Instagram messages, WhatsApp and live chat.
  • Workflows and automations, calendars and appointment reminders.
  • Reputation management and automated review requests.

Why your agency may mention it

The home page says HighLevel was built and powered by marketers before it was introduced to the market, and its pricing includes a plan with unlimited sub-accounts. In practice, an agency on HighLevel often holds the main account and sets up each client as a sub-account inside it.

That matters when you part ways. HighLevel's sub-account transfer guide says a transfer moves an entire sub-account from one HighLevel agency to another, with contacts and conversations kept together, and that by default only the Agency Owner can request or complete one. As general advice, that means your records may sit in an account someone else controls, so agree in writing how you get them back.

Questions to ask before your leads run on it

  • Whose account is it in, and will someone at your business have an admin login? HighLevel's export guide says only admin users can export contacts.
  • Can you export every contact to a spreadsheet file at any time? The same guide says an export includes names, email addresses, phone numbers, tags and any custom fields you select, but not automation history such as past emails and text logs, so ask how that history would be kept.
  • If you leave, will you get a full export, and how fast? A transfer, as HighLevel's guide describes it, moves a sub-account to another HighLevel agency account, which it says must be active and eligible, so it only helps if you or your next agency has one.
  • Who owns the phone numbers inside it, and can they move with you? The transfer guide says whether numbers move automatically depends on the phone provider both agencies use.
  • Are automatic texts and calls sent only with the person's consent? The related answer on texting a new lead covers what consent means.
  • Is every lead tagged with its source and followed to won, lost or still open?

Do you need it?

Not as such. The tool is not a result: what counts is that every inquiry is answered fast, followed up and recorded with its source. Many small teams can do that with a simpler CRM or a well-kept spreadsheet, as the related answer on what a CRM is explains. If an agency runs your leads on HighLevel, judge it by inquiries answered and jobs won, and by whether you could leave with your records.
