Can AI tools do your SEO for you?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Partly. An AI tool can help an owner draft pages, list the questions customers ask and tidy wording, but it cannot know the business's real facts, earn honest reviews or mentions, or see which inquiries came from search. Google says appropriate use of AI is not against its guidelines, while using it to churn out many pages without adding value can break its spam policies. Treat AI as help for someone who knows the business, not a replacement.

What an AI tool can help with

For a single page, the related answer on whether AI can write your website covers what a tool can draft and what it gets wrong; the help below is the rest of the job.

  • A first draft of a page, built from your own notes; the related answer on whether AI can write your website covers copy in detail.
  • A first draft of a reply to a review, which you edit before it goes out.
  • Explaining an unfamiliar term in a marketing report, which you then check.

What it cannot do for you

  • Earn reviews or mentions. Those come from real customers and other businesses, and no tool can earn them for you.
  • Stand in for a real person. Google's helpful content guidance calls fabricated creator profiles, such as ones using AI-generated headshots or made-up names, a form of deception.
  • Tell you which inquiries came from search. Only your own lead records can.
  • Promise a ranking. Google says to be wary of tools that promise ranking success: "No third-party tool has access to our internal ranking or AI systems."

What Google says about pages made with AI

The related answer on whether AI can write your website covers Google's 2023 post on AI content and its generative AI guidance. Three further points matter when the question is the whole job, not one page. The FAQ in that 2023 post says "Appropriate use of AI or automation is not against our guidelines." Its spam policies describe scaled content abuse as generating many pages mainly to manipulate rankings rather than to help users, say the practice is typically large amounts of unoriginal content of little value "no matter how it's created", and give using generative AI to make many pages without adding value as one example. And its 2026 guide to AI search advises against content that simply restates others or "could easily be produced by a generative AI model".

When paying for help still makes sense

As general advice, AI can make an owner faster at tasks the owner already understands. Outside help usually pays when the work keeps slipping, spans the website, profiles and lead tracking at once, or needs to be judged by inquiries rather than by pages produced. The related answer on how much marketing to do yourself helps decide where that line sits, and the related answer on how much time SEO takes from an owner covers the part only you can supply.
