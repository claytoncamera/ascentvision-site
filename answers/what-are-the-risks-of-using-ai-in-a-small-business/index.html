What are the risks of using AI in a small business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

The main risks are wrong answers, private data sent to an outside company, a customer misled, a promise made in your name, copied content, and a tool you come to depend on that later changes. Each can be limited: a person checking what customers see, a rule about what goes in, written boundaries, and a way to switch it off. A limit lowers a risk but does not remove it.

General advice before you rely on a tool

This section is general advice, not drawn from the sources cited here. Before a tool touches real work, have a second person look at what it produces, read what its plan does with what you type, and decide who in the business may use it. Messages and campaigns deserve a second look in particular, because AI-written text can read as generic or get a detail about a customer wrong. For a question about copyright, privacy law or a contract, ask a lawyer; this is general information, not legal advice.

What NIST adds

NIST says it developed a framework, released on January 26, 2023, to better manage risks to individuals, organizations and society associated with AI, and that it is "intended for voluntary use". So NIST presents it as voluntary guidance, not a requirement. Its Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, released on July 26, 2024, can help organizations identify unique risks posed by generative AI, and NIST says version 1.0 of the framework is being revised. The table above is this answer's own, not NIST's.

What none of this removes

A limit lowers a risk; it does not remove it. Keep a person on anything a customer may hold you to, and check a sample of the tool's work often. The related answer on where to start with AI keeps the first task small.
