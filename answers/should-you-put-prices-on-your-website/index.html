Should a service business put prices on its website?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

It depends on how predictable your work is. Showing prices tends to screen out people who were never going to buy, and it answers the first question most visitors have. But when every job is different, a single number can mislead or scare off good customers. Many service businesses do well with a middle ground: a starting-from range, a plain list of what changes the price, and an example of a typical job.

The case for showing prices

  • Fewer inquiries from people whose budget does not fit, so less time on calls that go nowhere.
  • Visitors who do get in touch have already accepted the rough cost, which often makes the first conversation easier.
  • It reads as open and confident. Visitors who find no hint of cost may move on without asking.
  • For simple, repeatable services, a clear price can be the reason someone books instead of moving on.

The case against

  • Quotes that depend on size, condition, materials or location are hard to put into one number.
  • A low starting figure can attract people expecting the minimum, and a high one can scare off buyers who needed less.
  • Prices change, and a page that is out of date causes awkward conversations.
  • Competitors can see exactly where you sit.

A middle ground that often works

You do not have to choose between a full price list and nothing. Options that give visitors something useful without boxing you in:

  • A starting-from figure or a typical range for each service.
  • A short list of what affects the price, such as size, access, materials or urgency.
  • A sample scope: what a typical job includes, how long it takes and what the customer gets.
  • A clear next step, such as a short call or a form that asks for the details you need to quote.

How to decide

Look at your last few months of inquiries. If many ended the moment price came up, showing a range will likely save you time. If most jobs need a visit or a conversation before anyone can quote, explain your process clearly instead and make the next step easy.

Ascent Vision does not publish prices; every engagement is scoped on a call. Whichever way you go, say plainly how pricing works, so no visitor has to guess.
