Why does ChatGPT recommend my competitor and not me?

Ascent Vision · Published October 20, 2026

Usually because the assistant found it easier to say what your competitor does, where and for whom. No outsider can see exactly why one business is named over another, but the differences an owner can see tend to be plain ones: a clearer description of the work, more recent reviews, more mentions on other sites, or the exact service the customer asked about named in so many words. Which of those weighs, no outsider knows.

What an assistant has to go on

An assistant can only describe a business from what has been written about it; the related answer on how customers find businesses through AI assistants covers where that writing comes from.

OpenAI says ChatGPT ranks search results "using multiple factors" meant to help people find relevant, reliable information, and that "Placement is not guaranteed". Responses that use web search may include citations a reader can open. Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in its AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond regular search. The point that clearly described businesses are easier to name is general advice, not something either company states.

Differences an owner can usually see

  • A clearer description. The competitor's site often says plainly what they do, in which towns and for whom, where another site only says "quality service".
  • The exact service. When a customer asked for a water heater replacement, the business that was named often says that job in so many words, not only "plumbing".
  • Recent reviews. The named business often has recent reviews that mention the actual job, rather than an older pile of short ones.
  • Mentions elsewhere. It is often written about on other sites too, such as a local news story or a trade association page. Which of these weighs, and how much, no one outside knows.

What no one outside can know

OpenAI does not say how its factors are weighed, and Google says a page meeting all of its requirements is still not guaranteed to be shown. Answers can also differ by person: OpenAI says ChatGPT may use an approximate location based on a person's IP address for local results, and saved memories, if turned on, may shape the search it runs. The competitor you see named may not be the one a customer across town sees. Anyone who claims to know the exact reason, or promises to make an assistant switch to you, is claiming more than can be known.

What you can do about it

Start by seeing the problem clearly: the related answer on checking what AI says about your business walks through asking the questions your customers ask and noting who gets named. OpenAI also says search results and citations "can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect"; if an assistant describes you wrongly, the related answer on whether AI assistants get facts wrong covers that case.

Beyond that, the owner's part is usually plain: say clearly what you do and where, name the services people ask for, ask happy customers for honest reviews without offering anything in return, and do work worth being mentioned for. None of it guarantees a mention, but it gives an assistant something accurate to say.
