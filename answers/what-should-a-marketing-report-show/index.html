What should a marketing report show a small business owner?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

A good monthly marketing report fits on one page and answers four questions: how many inquiries and customers came in, and from which sources; what each source cost; what was done this month and what changed because of it; and what happens next. Traffic and rankings can appear, but only as supporting detail under inquiries and customers.

What belongs on the page

  • Inquiries and customers won this month, by source, next to last month.
  • What each source cost, and the cost per new customer.
  • Time to first reply on new inquiries.
  • What was done this month, in plain words.
  • What changed: where you now show up, or are mentioned, that you did not before.
  • What happens next month, and what the agency needs from you.

Where the numbers should come from

Every number should trace back to your own lead records. Sources should use the same eight names every month: search, AI answer, social, referral, paid, email, campaign and direct. Each inquiry should end as won, lost or still open. A returning customer, or someone who typed your address, counts as direct, so a report should not credit them to a paid source.

The U.S. Small Business Administration's marketing guide has a section called "Measure and update your plan". It asks you to "compare your marketing and sales costs to the revenue it generates". As general advice, a report that cannot support that comparison is incomplete.

A layout that works

Put three numbers at the top, each next to last month: inquiries, customers won and cost per customer. Below them, one row per source. At the bottom, one short paragraph on what changed and one line on what happens next. Ask for the list of inquiries behind the numbers, so you can check a few of them against your own phone and email.

Warning signs

  • Pages of charts and no mention of inquiries or customers.
  • Numbers you cannot trace back to real inquiries.
  • A different set of measures every month.
  • Credit for customers who would have found you anyway, such as repeat customers or people who already knew your name.

What you should be able to do with it

Read it in five minutes, check any number against your own records, and make one decision: keep, change or stop. If a report does not let you do that, ask for one that does. The related answer on customer acquisition cost, ROI and ROAS explains the cost terms a report may use.

Sources
