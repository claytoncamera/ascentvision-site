How do you tell whether a new lead is a good fit?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

A good-fit lead needs what you actually offer, is somewhere you serve, wants it on a timeline you can meet, and expects something you can deliver. A few short questions on the first reply or call will usually tell you, and asking them early saves both sides time. Write the answers next to the lead so the next person who picks it up does not have to ask again.

Questions that usually tell you

  • What are you looking for help with?
  • Where are you, or where is the work?
  • When do you need it?
  • What would a good result look like for you?
  • Have you worked with someone on this before, and how did it go?

What the answers usually mean

Good fitAsk moreProbably not a fit
The needMatches a service you are good atUnclear what they want yetWork you do not do
The placeIn your areaNear the edge of itOutside it
The timingOne you can meetFlexible or vagueSooner than you can honestly deliver
The resultSomething you can deliverNot sure yetSomething you cannot honestly promise

How to ask without sounding like a form

Ask one question at a time, as part of the conversation, and say why you are asking: "So I can give you a real answer, where is the work?" People usually answer readily when they can see the question helps them. Skip any question whose answer you would not act on.

Saying no kindly

If it is not a fit, say so quickly and politely, and point them somewhere better if you can. Reply the way they contacted you, and text only with the person's consent. A fast, honest no can leave a good impression, and sometimes comes back as a referral. The related answer on saying no to a customer has sample lines.

Record the fit and the outcome separately

Google Ads Help, written for advertisers, treats a "qualified" lead and a "converted" lead as separate steps, and describes converted ones as having "completed a chosen step in the lead conversion process". A small business can borrow the idea without any ad account: note whether each lead was a fit, and separately whether it was won, lost or still open. A good fit can be lost, and a poor fit can be won. Over a few months the two notes show which sources bring good-fit leads and which bring mostly the wrong ones.
