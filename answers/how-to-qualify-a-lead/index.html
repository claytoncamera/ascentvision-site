How do you tell whether a new lead is a good fit?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

A good-fit lead needs what you actually offer, is somewhere you serve, wants it on a timeline you can meet, and expects something you can deliver. A few short questions on the first reply or call will usually tell you, and asking them early saves both sides time.

Questions that usually tell you

  • What are you looking for help with?
  • Where are you, or where is the work?
  • When do you need it?
  • What would a good result look like for you?
  • Have you worked with someone on this before, and how did it go?

Signs of a good fit

  • The need matches a service you are good at.
  • The timing and location work for you.
  • Their idea of a good result is something you can honestly deliver.
  • They are ready to talk about next steps.

Saying no kindly

If it is not a fit, say so quickly and politely, and point them somewhere better if you can. A fast, honest no can leave a good impression, and sometimes comes back as a referral.

Record the outcome

Note whether each lead was a fit and why not when it was not. Over a few months, that shows which sources bring good-fit leads and which bring mostly the wrong ones.
