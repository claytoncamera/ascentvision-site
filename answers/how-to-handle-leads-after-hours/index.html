How should a small business handle inquiries that arrive after hours?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

Acknowledge every after-hours inquiry right away with an automatic reply that thanks the person and says exactly when a real reply will come, such as by 9 the next morning. Then keep that promise: make overnight inquiries the first task of the next working day, and set a clear weekend rule so no one waits until Monday without knowing it.

Why speed still matters after hours

The first reply matters most, and the related answer on how fast to reply to a new lead covers why. At night a full reply within the hour is often not possible. An honest acknowledgement is, and it tells the customer someone will deal with them.

What the automatic reply should say

  • Thanks for getting in touch, and confirmation that the message arrived.
  • A specific time for a real reply, not "as soon as possible".
  • What to do if it is urgent, if you offer urgent help.
  • The business name and a way to reach you.
  • Nothing else. No sales pitch, no attachments.
  • If you send it by text, do so only when the person has given consent to receive texts from you, for example by ticking a clear box on your form.

Promise only what you can keep

Pick a reply time you can hit every day, even on a busy morning. If you sometimes cannot, promise a later time and beat it. A promise that is often missed usually does more harm than no promise at all.

A morning-first routine

  • Before any other work, open every inquiry that arrived overnight.
  • Reply in the order they came in.
  • Answer the question they asked and offer one clear next step.
  • Record where each inquiry came from and what happened.
  • If you cannot answer fully yet, say when you will.

A weekend rule

Decide once and write it down. Either someone checks inquiries once a day at weekends, or the automatic reply says plainly that replies come on Monday morning. Either can work, as long as the customer knows what to expect. Silence is what usually costs the job.
