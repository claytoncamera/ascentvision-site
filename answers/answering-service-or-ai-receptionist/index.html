Should a small business use an answering service or an AI receptionist?

Ascent Vision · Published October 16, 2026

Either can beat voicemail, which many callers may skip. A human answering service usually handles unusual or upset callers better; an AI receptionist answers at once, at any hour, but can say wrong things in your name. Pick the one you can check. Whichever you use, every call should land in your lead list with its source, and calls should be recorded only with the person's consent.

The three options side by side

What any option must do

  • Take the same details on every call: name, how to reach them, what they need and how they heard about you.
  • Get every call into your lead list the same day, with its source: search, AI answer, social, referral, paid, email, campaign or direct.
  • Pass urgent calls to a person at once, using a written rule for what counts as urgent, such as a leak, no heat or a customer locked out.
  • Record calls only with the person's consent.

Which to choose

If most calls are simple, such as booking a visit, leaving details or asking your hours, an AI receptionist can often handle them, provided someone reads its call summaries every day. If calls often need judgment, involve emergencies or come from upset customers, a human service is usually the safer choice. If you miss only a few calls a week and can call back within the hour, a clear voicemail greeting and a fast callback may be enough. The related answer on handling after-hours inquiries covers what to promise callers.

Check it before you trust it

Call it yourself a few times with real questions, including one it should not answer, and listen to what it says in your name. Then check that every call reached your lead list and ends with an outcome: won, lost or still open.
