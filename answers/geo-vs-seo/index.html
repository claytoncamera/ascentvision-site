GEO vs SEO: what is the difference, and do you need both?

Ascent Vision · Published October 14, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

SEO (search engine optimization) aims to get a business a ranked link on a search results page. GEO (generative engine optimization) aims to get it mentioned inside an answer written by an AI assistant. They share most of their foundations but are measured differently, so a business can do well in one and be absent from the other. Most service businesses are probably better off working on both.

The difference in one table

SEOGEO
GoalA ranked link on a results pageA mention or recommendation inside an AI answer
Where it showsGoogle and other search enginesChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and other assistants
What the customer seesA list of links to choose fromA written answer, often naming only a few businesses
How you know it workedVisits and inquiries from searchMentions in AI answers and inquiries from them

Where they overlap

AI assistants and search engines read the same web, so the foundations are shared. For its own AI features, Google says there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode". Its guide to generative AI search says that, from Google Search's perspective, optimizing for it is still SEO. Those statements cover Google's products, not ChatGPT or other assistants. The related answer on AEO, GEO and AI SEO covers the labels.

Where they differ

A results page offers a page of choices; an AI answer usually names far fewer. The answer is written, so how a business is described matters as much as whether it appears. And because an assistant may name a business without anyone clicking, success is measured in mentions and in the inquiries that follow, not only in visits. The related answer on AI answers and clicks covers the evidence so far.

What the research says

A research paper proposing GEO reports that it "can boost visibility by up to 40% in generative engine responses" on the authors' own test queries. That is a research result, not a promise for any one business, and no one can promise that an assistant will name a particular business.

Do you need both?

For most service businesses, probably. Search is still where many customers start, and AI answers are where some may now start. Tag every lead as coming from search or from an AI answer, and after a few months you can see which one is paying off for your business. The related answer on telling whether a customer found you through ChatGPT shows how to ask.
