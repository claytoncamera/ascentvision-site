How do you cut spam calls to your business line without missing real customers?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Filter the calls your phone or carrier already flags as likely spam, but avoid settings that silence every unknown number: on a business line, new customers are usually unknown numbers. Screening, where the phone asks a caller who they are before it rings, is a safer middle ground. Do not count on the national Do Not Call registry for a business line: the FCC's rule protects registered residential subscribers, and the FTC's telemarketing rule exempts calls to businesses, with some exceptions. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Why silencing unknown numbers costs customers

Apple's iPhone guide describes a Silence setting under which calls from unsaved numbers are silenced, sent to voicemail and shown in Recents. On a personal phone that can be fine. On the phone customers call, a first-time caller is usually an unsaved number, and some will try the next business rather than leave a message, so as general advice keep silencing off on that line.

Tools that screen instead of block

  • iPhone: Apple says Call Screening answers calls from unknown numbers, and after the caller gives their name and reason, the phone rings and shows the response so you can decide whether to pick up. A separate Spam filter silences calls your carrier identifies as potential spam or fraud and moves them to a Spam list.
  • Google's Phone app: Google says caller ID and spam protection is on by default and can warn about potential spam callers. An optional Filter spam calls setting blocks them: you get no missed-call or voicemail notifications, but the calls stay in your call history and you can still check voicemail.
  • Pixel phones: Google says Call Screen lets you find out who is calling and why before you pick up, and can screen calls automatically on Pixel phones in the US.
  • Mistakes happen: Google lets you mark a call wrongly labeled spam as Not spam, and Apple lets you mark an unknown caller as known.

What the Do Not Call registry does and does not do

The FCC's do-not-call rule, in 47 CFR 64.1200 as published by Cornell's Legal Information Institute, bars telephone solicitations to a residential telephone subscriber who has registered on the national registry. The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule, in 16 CFR 310.6, exempts calls between a telemarketer and any business to get the business to buy goods or services or make a charitable contribution, with exceptions that include calls to sell nondurable office or cleaning supplies.

This is a plain summary, not legal advice. It covers only those two federal provisions, not state rules, robocall rules or whether any particular call to you is lawful.

A daily routine that keeps real customers

  • Check voicemail and the spam and unknown-caller lists at set times each day. Call back the real customers who asked you to, and text only with the person's consent.
  • Save regular customers and suppliers as contacts, so filters treat them as known.
  • Use a greeting that asks for a name, number and the job, as the related answer on what a voicemail greeting should say describes.
  • If your own outgoing calls get labeled, the related answer on why your business calls show as spam covers that side.
