How do you know if a customer found you through ChatGPT?

Ascent Vision · Published October 20, 2026

Usually the only reliable way is to ask. Many people who hear about a business from ChatGPT or another assistant never click a link: they read the name, then search for it, type the web address or call. Those visits tend to look like direct visits or ordinary searches. Ask every new inquiry how they found you, write the answer down next to the lead, and AI answers become a channel you can count.

Why AI referrals often hide

OpenAI says ChatGPT responses that use web search may include citations, and that selecting a citation opens its source. That is one way a person can land on your site straight from ChatGPT, but many read the name and move on. They might search it later, type the web address from memory or simply call. Nothing about that visit or call says an assistant started it, so it tends to be counted as direct or as a search.

Google adds a wrinkle of its own: it says clicks from its AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted with its ordinary search traffic. As general advice, that means a visit that began with a Google AI answer usually cannot be told apart from an ordinary search visit. And because OpenAI says ChatGPT may use an approximate location for local results, a customer may never have typed your town at all, which makes the path even harder to guess from the outside.

The one question to ask

Ask every new inquiry how they found you, in the first call or reply, and write the answer down. A plain "How did you hear about us?" usually works. If the answer is vague, a short follow-up such as "Did you search online, ask an AI assistant, see us on social or hear from someone?" helps people remember. People do not always recall exactly, so their own words are worth keeping next to the lead.

Where the answer goes

Record each lead's channel as one of: search, AI answer, social, referral, paid, email, campaign or direct, and later its outcome: won, lost or still open. The related answer on lead attribution explains why that record matters. A customer who asked ChatGPT and then searched your name touched two channels; the related answer on which source gets credit for a customer covers how to count that.

What the count can and cannot tell you

Asking will usually still undercount AI answers, since some people forget or say "online". The count is most useful as a trend over months: whether AI answer leads appear at all, whether they grow, and whether they are won as often as leads from other channels. A small number of real, recorded AI answer leads usually says more than a guess about a large one.
