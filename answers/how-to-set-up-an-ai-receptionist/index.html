How do you set up an AI receptionist for a small business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

Set up an AI receptionist in this order: list the call types and hours it will cover, write the greeting and the facts it may state, write the hand-off and emergency rules and name who is on call, keep the phone number in the business's name and forward calls to the service, connect the calendar and lead list, test it with real calls, then run it with daily summaries and a review date. The rules come first, before any real caller reaches it.

Steps one to four: decide on paper first

  • Step one, the calls and hours. List the kinds of calls it will take and when: after hours only, overflow when no one picks up in time, or all day. Starting with after hours or overflow usually limits what a mistake can touch.
  • Step two, the greeting and the fact sheet. Write what it says first (the related answer on how an AI receptionist works covers what to tell callers before you switch it on). Then write the facts it may state, and decide what it says for anything not on the sheet.
  • Step three, the hand-off and emergency rules. Write when it passes a call to a person and what it tells the caller meanwhile. The related answer on which calls an AI receptionist should take has the rules themselves.
  • Step four, who is on call. Name one person and a backup; the related answer on which calls an AI receptionist should take covers how the hand-off works. A rule with nobody behind it can be a weak point.

Steps five to seven: connect it and test it

  • Step five, the number. Keep it on an account in the business's name and make sure you can switch the forwarding off yourself. The related answer on who should own your business phone number explains why.
  • Step six, the calendar and lead list. Connect only what it needs, in accounts you own: the calendar for the job types it may book, and the lead list so each call lands with a summary. If the calendar is not ready, start with take-a-message only and add booking later. The related answer on how an AI receptionist works covers why booking needs real availability.
  • Step seven, test calls. Have someone other than you call with an ordinary booking, an emergency, an upset caller, a question it should not answer and a request for a person. After each call, check what it said, whether the on-call person was alerted and whether the lead record is right. The related answer on answering services and AI receptionists also covers checking that every call reached your lead list.

Step eight: run it closely, then review

For the first weeks, read the call summaries daily, as the related answer on which calls it should take describes. Change one thing at a time and repeat the test call that matters.

Put the first review on the calendar before you go live, for example a few weeks out. At that review, decide whether to widen its job, narrow it or keep it as it is. Widen slowly, one call type at a time.

Consent and disclosure

Callbacks, texts and recordings go out only with the person's consent; the related answers on how an AI receptionist works, consent to record customer calls and telling customers you use AI cover what applies.
