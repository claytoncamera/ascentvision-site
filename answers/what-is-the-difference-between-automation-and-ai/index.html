What is the difference between automation and AI?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

Automation follows fixed rules you set: when this happens, do that. AI interprets, for example by reading a free-written message and working out what it means, or by drafting a reply. Rules are usually cheaper, more predictable and easier to check. AI can handle messy input but can be wrong in new ways. Many useful systems combine the two.

One example, two ways

A customer fills in your form. With fixed rules, a box that says "repair" sends the inquiry to one person and triggers a standard reply. Every step was written by someone, and the same input gives the same result.

With AI, the customer instead writes, "the garage opener stopped working after the storm." A model reads the sentence, guesses that it is a repair request and can draft a reply. Nobody wrote a rule for that exact wording, which is its strength and also its risk, because it may guess wrong.

Which to choose

Start with rules wherever the input is predictable and the steps rarely change. If a rule can do the job, it is usually the cheaper, safer and easier option to check.

Bring in AI for the step where rules cannot cope, such as free-written messages or a first draft. Keep a person on anything a customer will read; the related answer on whether a person should approve what AI sends to customers covers when.

Many systems use both

Ask any seller which steps are fixed rules and which use an AI model. Labels vary, and a simple rule-based tool is sometimes described as AI. The related answer on AI agents covers systems that choose their own steps, and the related answer on what a small business can automate with AI lists the tasks that tend to fit.

  • A rule records each inquiry and tags where it came from.
  • AI reads the message, suggests the type of job and drafts a reply.
  • A rule sends the inquiry to the right person, and sends it to a person for a look when the AI is unsure.
  • A person approves the reply before it goes out.
