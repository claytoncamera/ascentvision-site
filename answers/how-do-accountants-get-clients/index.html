How do accountants and bookkeepers get clients?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Accountants and bookkeepers usually get clients through referrals first, then through directories a buyer may check, such as a state CPA society, QuickBooks ProAdvisor and Xero. Tax season changes what a website should offer. In Florida, the statute bars knowingly using the title CPA without an active license or practice privileges. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Referrals come first

An accountant handles private financial records, so a recommendation from someone the buyer already trusts often counts for more than an advertisement. The related answer on referral programs covers asking at the end of a job and thanking people the same way every time. Record each new inquiry's source as referral, with the name of whoever sent them, so you can see which relationships bring clients.

Directories a buyer may check

The Florida Institute of CPAs directory lets a buyer connect with a CPA by county, industries served, services offered and languages spoken. It adds that the listings come from members and do not represent all members or firms, or an endorsement. QuickBooks' directory is headed "Find an accountant or bookkeeper near you with QuickBooks ProAdvisor" and lists ProAdvisors, whom it calls "independent accounting professionals". Xero has a similar advisor directory and says its listed firms have "staff members trained in Xero".

As general advice, join the directories that fit the software you work in and the clients you want, then check in your own lead records whether any of them brings inquiries. The related answer on lead attribution explains how to record where each inquiry came from.

Local Services Ads and this trade

Google's page on getting started with Local Services Ads says the ads are available only for certain service categories, and it lists them. As of October 2026, the list for the United States includes "Tax services" and "Financial planning services" but has no entry named accountant or bookkeeper. A business that does tax work may find the ads open to it, while a bookkeeper may not. The page points to an eligibility check for your area, which is worth running before anyone plans around the ads.

Tax season changes the offer

Before the filing deadline, many visitors want to know one thing: whether you can take them on, and by when. Afterward, they may be looking for year-round bookkeeping or planning. Change the first screen of the homepage and its next step for each period, and say plainly when you are full. The related answer on what a service business homepage should say covers the first screen.

The title rule in Florida

Section 473.322 of the 2026 Florida Statutes says a person may not knowingly use the titles "certified public accountant" or "public accountant", the abbreviation C.P.A., or similar wording, unless the person holds an active license under that chapter or has practice privileges. Another paragraph of the same section says it does not prohibit people other than CPAs from performing other services that use accounting skills, including preparing tax returns and preparing financial statements without expressing an opinion. It makes a violation a first-degree misdemeanor.

A bookkeeper or tax preparer who is not a CPA can describe the work in plain words and leave the title out. Other states usually have their own rules. This is a plain summary, not legal advice; for a specific situation, ask a Florida attorney.
