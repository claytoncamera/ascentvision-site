What should you tell a marketing agency before it starts?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 31, 2026

Before any work starts, give the agency a one-page brief: what you sell and to whom, the areas you serve, what a typical customer is worth to you, what has worked and what has not, where inquiries come from today, what you will and will not do yourself, and who approves work. A clear brief can save weeks of guessing, and it gives both sides something to judge the work against later.

The one-page brief

One page in plain words is usually enough; update it when the business changes. The related answer on what a customer is worth shows how to work out the value of a job.

The discovery call

Most agencies open with a discovery call: a conversation, before any proposal, in which the agency asks about the business, its customers, what has been tried and what a good result would look like. Go in with the one-page brief above and expect the questions in the next section. It is also your interview: ask who would do the work, what you would receive each month, and what the agency would need from you.

A discovery call should cost you nothing but time. Some agencies then offer a paid review of your website or marketing before a proposal, which can be fair when its scope and fee are in writing. The related answers on what an SEO proposal should include and on the questions to ask before hiring an SEO agency cover what comes next.

What a good agency will ask anyway

Google's guide "Do you need an SEO?" says to see whether an SEO is interested in you and your business, and that "Your SEO should ask questions about what makes your business unique", who your competitors are, how search results can help and how your customers find you.

As general advice, a written brief answers most of that before the first call. Name your main competitors, and say what customers tell you sets you apart.

Access and ownership come first

Give access, not passwords: add the agency as a user on accounts that stay in the business's name. The related answers on who should own your domain name and who should own your ad accounts explain why and how.

The related answer on what to have before starting SEO covers the rest of the setup, and the related answer on questions to ask before hiring an SEO agency covers checking the agency itself. The brief is the handover between the two.

Agree how the work will be judged

Put these in writing next to the brief:

  • The few numbers you will both watch, starting with inquiries by source and whether each was won, lost or still open.
  • What the agency must show you before anything is published in your name.
  • How often you will hear from it, and who your contact is.
  • What it needs from you each month, and by when.
