How long should your first marketing contract be?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

There is no single right length, but a fair first marketing contract is often short or easy to leave: a minimum period tied to the set-up work, then month to month, with a clear notice period and no automatic renewal you can miss. Some owners start with one paid project before any ongoing agreement. A longer term is easier to accept when the agency can say what the time pays for and what you keep if you leave.

Why agencies ask for a minimum

Agencies often give two reasons. The first weeks can be mostly set-up work, done before there is anything to show. And search work can take months to show up in inquiries; the related answer on how long SEO takes covers that. Both can be fair reasons for a minimum period. Neither is a reason, on its own, for a long lock-in.

Ways to set the term

These are common shapes for a first agreement, not a standard. The related answer on what a marketing retainer is explains how a monthly arrangement differs from a one-off project.

Questions to ask about the term

The related answer on what a marketing agency contract should say lists the clauses to look for, including term and renewal and ending it. These questions are only about length.

  • What exactly does the minimum period pay for, and what will I hold at the end of it?
  • If agreed work is not delivered, can I leave early, and how is that decided?

When a longer term can make sense

A longer term can be reasonable when most of the agreed work comes early, such as a large build, or when you have seen the agency work and are comfortable with how it reports. As general advice, the longer the term, the more specific the written scope, the exit and the ownership of your accounts should be.

This is general advice, not legal advice. A lawyer can read a contract you are unsure about.
