How do you write a good first reply to a new inquiry?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

A good first reply to a new inquiry does four things in a few short sentences: it thanks the person by name, answers the question they actually asked, offers one clear next step, and says when that step will happen. It should read as if a person wrote it for them, not like a template, and it should go out quickly.

The structure

PartWhat to write
ThankUse their name and mention what they asked about, so they know you read it.
AnswerAnswer their actual question in a sentence or two. If you need more detail, say what and why.
One next stepOffer one thing to do next, such as a short call or a visit, with two specific times to choose from.
WhenSay when you will follow up if they do not reply, and then do it.

A short example

  • "Hi Maria, thanks for getting in touch about the leak under your kitchen sink."
  • "Yes, that is something we fix often. To give you a firm answer, we would need a quick look in person."
  • "I can come by Thursday at 10 or Friday at 2. Which suits you better?"
  • "If neither works, reply with a time that does, and I will confirm it by the end of today."
  • "Thanks, Sam, [business name], [phone number]"

Mistakes that cost jobs

  • Attachments. Brochures and documents are rarely opened on a phone, and they make the reply feel automated.
  • Price dumps. Sending a full price list before you understand the job answers a question they did not ask and often invites comparison on price alone.
  • Generic templates. A reply that never mentions their question tells them they are one of many.
  • Several options at once. Too many choices often means no choice.
  • No time frame. "Let me know" leaves the next move to them, and many will not make it.
  • Asking for details they already gave you.

Templates are fine as a starting point

Keeping a saved outline saves time and keeps replies consistent. Just rewrite the first two lines for each person, so the reply starts with their name and their question. That small edit is usually what makes a reply feel personal.
