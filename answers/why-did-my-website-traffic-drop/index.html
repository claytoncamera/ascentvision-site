Why did my website traffic drop, and should I worry?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

A drop in website traffic usually has an ordinary cause, so check the plain ones first: the time of year, a recent change to the site, anything that broke, and a shift in Google's results. Compare with the same weeks last year, then look at inquiries. If calls and forms held steady, the drop may matter little; if they fell too, it needs attention.

Start with the calendar

Compare the weeks that dropped with the same weeks last year. Holidays, school breaks, weather and trends often move demand for many services. Google's own guide to debugging traffic drops says traffic can fall because of "seasonality throughout the year", and it suggests looking back far enough to see whether a drop happens every year because of a festivity or a trend.

If last year shows the same dip, the site probably did nothing wrong.

Then check your own site

Work from the easiest checks to the hardest. Is the site up and quick on a phone? The related answers on a website being down and on a slow website cover both. Do the contact form and the phone number still work? Was anything changed just before the drop, such as a redesign, a new domain or new page addresses? If so, the related answer on whether a new website or domain hurts your Google results covers what to expect.

It also says that malware or phishing can lead Google to show warnings before people reach a site, which may decrease traffic. The related answer on a new website or domain covers what a rebuild can do.

Then the counting and the wider web

Sometimes the visits did not fall, the counting did. A rebuild or a cookie banner change can stop a site from recording some visits, so ask whoever runs the site whether that changed.

Google changes its results from time to time. Its guide points to the Google Search Status Dashboard for notable updates, and the ranking history there lists updates by date, including a September 2026 spam update; a listed update need not have touched your site, and Google's guide says that after an update there might not be anything fundamentally wrong with your content. It also says "Small fluctuations in position can happen at any time", and it recommends against radical changes to a page that already performs well. AI answers can also take clicks, as the related answer on AI answers and clicks describes. A new competitor is another possible cause that a visit count alone will not show.

Decide with inquiries, not visits

Visits are a supporting number. What decides whether to worry is whether calls, forms and booked jobs fell with them, which the related answer on traffic without customers explains. Ask whoever runs the site:

  • What changed on the site, and on what date?
  • Do the form, the phone link and the booking button all work today?
  • Is the site still recording every visit, and since when?
  • How do inquiries by source compare with the same weeks last year?
