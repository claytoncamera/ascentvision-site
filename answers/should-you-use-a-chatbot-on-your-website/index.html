Should a small business use a chatbot on its website?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

Only if it helps visitors get in touch faster than your form and phone already do. A chatbot can answer simple questions and take details out of hours, but a bad one gets in the way, gives wrong answers in your name, and loses leads. For many small businesses, a short form and a fast human reply are often enough.

When it can help

  • You get many repeat questions with short, fixed answers.
  • Many visitors arrive out of hours and need to leave their details.
  • Someone checks what it says and follows up on every conversation.

When it gets in the way

  • It covers the page or the button on a phone.
  • It answers questions it should not, such as prices, availability or advice, and gets them wrong.
  • Conversations are not passed to a person or recorded with the other leads.
  • It pretends to be a person.

Before you add one

  • Decide what it may answer and what it must hand to a person.
  • Say clearly that it is automated, and how conversations are stored.
  • Make sure every conversation that is an inquiry lands in the same place as your other leads, with its source.
  • If it collects a phone number, say what the number will be used for, and call or text only with the person's consent.
  • Read a sample of its conversations every week for the first month.

What two U.S. agencies say

The U.S. Small Business Administration's Manage your business guide, in its section on AI for small businesses, lists a website chatbot under improving customer service: "Add a website chatbot that can answer common questions or complete an order". That is a suggestion, not a requirement.

In a 2024 release about actions over deceptive claims for AI tools, then-FTC Chair Lina M. Khan said the actions show "no AI exemption from the laws on the books". The cases listed include a complaint alleging a company promoted an AI tool that enabled its customers to create fake reviews and a service sold as an "AI Lawyer". As general advice, treat whatever a chatbot tells customers as a statement from your business. The related answer on telling customers you use AI covers disclosure. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Sources

  1. U.S. Small Business Administration, Manage your business: marketing and sales
  2. Federal Trade Commission, "FTC Announces Crackdown on Deceptive AI Claims and Schemes" (2024-09-25)
