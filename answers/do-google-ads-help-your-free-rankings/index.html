Does paying for Google Ads help you rank higher in the free results?

Ascent Vision · Published October 19, 2026

No. Google says that because it only sells ad space, "no one can buy better placement in organic Search results", and that advertising with Google has no effect on a site's presence in its search results. Ads and the unpaid results are separate systems: running ads buys labeled ad slots for as long as you pay, and stopping them does not lower your free position. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling a myth.

What Google says

Google's explanation of how Search works says businesses can advertise in clearly marked ad sections, and then: "no one can buy better placement in organic Search results". It adds that advertisers get no special advantages or ranking boosts, and that for most searches it shows no ads at all.

Its guidance for site owners says the same thing from the other side. Advertising with Google "won't have any effect on your site's presence in our search results", and "Google never accepts money to include or rank sites in our search results". The two systems are run separately, and paying into one does not reach the other.

Why the myth persists

The belief usually comes from honest observation. A business starts ads, the phone rings more, and a few weeks later it seems to show up everywhere, so the owner concludes that the ads lifted the free results too. What happened is often simpler: the ad sat at the top of the page, more people visited and remembered the name, and some of them later searched for the business by name.

Sellers add to the confusion. Some bundle ads and SEO in one package and imply that one feeds the other, and some callers claiming to be Google say a business must advertise to stay visible. Google's own pages say neither is true, and the related answer on how to spot an SEO scam covers the rest of that script.

What ads can and cannot do

Ads can put a business at the top of the page for a search the same day, for as long as it keeps paying. That is useful while free visibility is still building, and the related answer on whether paid search is better than SEO weighs the two channels in detail. What ads cannot do is move the free result underneath them, and stopping ads does not push that result down.

How to see it for yourself

Your own lead records settle this better than any claim. If every inquiry is tagged with its source, you can see the paid and search channels side by side: ads bring paid inquiries while they run, and search inquiries carry on at their own pace whether the ads are on or off. The related answer on what lead attribution is explains the tagging. If you can afford to, pausing ads for a few weeks and watching the search channel is the plainest test there is.
