Why is my website's contact form not sending me emails?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Often the form still works and the message is simply not reaching you: the notification goes to an old or mistyped address, lands in spam or another folder, or stopped after a change to your email or website. Send a test from your phone, then check your inbox, your spam folder and the list of form entries in your website builder. If the entry is there but no email arrived, tell your builder exactly which test failed and when.

Start with a real test

Fill in the form on your phone, on mobile data, as a customer would, using a personal email address so you can see the reply side too. Note the time. Then check the inbox the form should send to, its spam or junk folder and any other folders or tabs, and, if your builder keeps one, its list of form submissions. Check that the thank-you message appeared; the related answer on what a website should say after an inquiry covers that page.

Common causes an owner can check

  • The notification goes to an old address, such as a former employee's or one on an old domain.
  • The address has a typo, or the mailbox is full.
  • Messages land in spam or are sorted into another folder. Mark them as not spam and add the sending address to your contacts.
  • Something changed: a new email provider, a domain move or a new host. The related answer on why business emails go to spam explains why a change can break a setup that worked.
  • An update or a new add-on broke the form itself.
  • A spam filter on the form is turning real people away.

Entries in the builder, but no email

If your test appears in the builder's list but never reached your inbox, the form is working and the email is the problem. Ask whoever runs your website or your email to fix the delivery, and until it is fixed, check the builder's list of entries every day so no one waits. If the test does not appear anywhere, the form is not submitting at all; ask your builder to look at it the same day, and tell them which phone and browser you used.

A backup, so no inquiry is lost

  • Show a phone number that can be tapped to call near the form, so people have another way in.
  • Put a test on the calendar. The related answer on what a contact form should ask suggests one every month; as general advice, test weekly for the first few weeks after any change.
  • If you find messages that never reached you, reply now, the way each person asked to be contacted, and call or text only with the person's consent.
  • Record each inquiry with its source as it arrives, so a gap in inquiries is noticed in days, not months.
