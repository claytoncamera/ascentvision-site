Are Facebook and Instagram lead ads worth it for a service business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 18, 2026

They can be, but judge them by the jobs they bring in, not by how many forms get filled in. Meta's lead ads with an instant form ask people who tap the ad on Facebook or Instagram to fill out a form. Meta says its default form type is designed to bring a larger number of leads. In practice, that can mean more people who were only mildly interested. Record every lead as paid, follow it to won, lost or still open, and compare the jobs won with what you spent.

How Meta's lead ads work

Meta's Business Help Center article "About lead ads" says lead ads are designed to help businesses find people who may be interested in their products or services and collect information from them. It describes three types: ads that encourage people to call, ads with an instant form, and ads that open a conversation in Messenger, Instagram or WhatsApp.

The same article says lead ads can appear in Facebook and Instagram feeds and Stories, in-stream reels, Marketplace and more. When people click the ad, they are prompted to fill out the form, and the business can then download the leads, view them in Meta Business Suite or send them to its own customer system.

Why an easy form can bring weaker leads

Meta's article "About instant form types" explains that the default, called More volume, makes the form quick to submit on a phone and is "designed to generate a larger number of leads". A Higher intent option adds a note that the business may follow up and a review screen before sending. Meta says these features help prevent submissions from people "who are only marginally interested", and that this option may make more sense when every lead needs follow-up or further qualification.

Meta's “About lead ads” article says instant forms can include custom questions. As general advice, not Meta's: a service business has to call or message each person, so a form that takes seconds to send can fill a list with people who barely remember sending it, and one custom question about the job or the town can help you sort leads before you reply.

Judge them by jobs won

Put every lead from these forms in your own lead list with paid as its source and the platform in the notes, not only in Meta's tools. Follow each to won, lost or still open. After a month or two, compare the jobs won and what they earned with what the ads cost. A cheap form fill that never turns into a job is not a good result. The related answer on buying leads from lead sites uses the same test for other paid sources.

Reply fast, and only with consent

Meta's "Best practices to respond to leads" recommends running lead ads during business hours so you can quickly start a conversation, and says that shortening the time to follow up can improve lead quality. Call or text a lead only with the person's consent. The related answer on how fast to reply covers why speed matters, and the related answer on tracking social media messages covers leads from the message type of ad.
