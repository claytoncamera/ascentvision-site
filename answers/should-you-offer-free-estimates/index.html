Should a service business offer free estimates?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

It depends on your work. Free estimates can bring in more inquiries, especially in trades where customers expect them, but every visit to a poor fit costs time you are not paid for. Two common middle grounds are a short screen of a few questions before any visit, and a paid assessment that is credited to the job if the customer goes ahead. Whatever you choose, say it plainly on your website.

The trade-offs

Free estimatesPaid assessment
InquiriesOften more, since asking costs the customer nothingOften fewer, but usually more serious
Your timeSpent on everyone, including people only comparing pricesPaid for, or at least better screened
FitCan include people who were never going to hire youTends to favor people ready to decide
Customer expectationCommon in many home-service tradesEasier to explain when the visit itself takes real expertise, such as finding a fault

Alternatives worth considering

  • A short screen before any visit: a few questions about the job, the area and the timing, by email, by form, or by call only with the person's consent. The related answer on how to tell whether a new lead is a good fit covers which questions.
  • A paid assessment credited to the job: the visit has a charge, and if the customer hires you, it comes off the final bill.
  • Free for some jobs, paid for others: free for simple, standard jobs, and paid when the visit involves diagnosis, testing or a written plan.
  • A rough range first, with a visit only for customers who want an exact price; the related answer on when a lead asks for the price first covers the conversation.

How to decide

Look at the last few months of estimate visits: how many turned into jobs, and how long each visit took, travel included. If most visits become work, free estimates may be paying for themselves. If many go nowhere, a screen or a paid assessment may save you real time.

It also helps to know what other businesses in your area offer, since customers often compare.

Say it clearly on your site

  • State the policy in one line near your contact button, such as "Free estimates for [type of job] in [area]" or "Assessments are charged and credited to the job if you go ahead."
  • Say what the estimate includes: a visit, a written quote, and roughly how long it takes.
  • If it is free, keep it free: no fee that appears later and no pressure to decide on the spot.
  • Say the same thing in your replies as on your site, so no one is surprised.
