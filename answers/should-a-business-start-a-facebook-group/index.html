Should a service business start a Facebook group, or just keep a page?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Usually, keep the Page first. Meta describes Pages as the way for a business to communicate with customers, run ads and see insights, while groups are spaces for approved members to talk about shared interests. A group can suit a service business whose customers want to talk to each other, such as a homeowners' tips group, but it takes steady time to moderate. Joining local groups is often enough.

Page and group, side by side

Meta's Business Help Center page on representing a business with Pages, profiles and groups compares them this way. It says all three are free, and that you need a personal profile before you can create a Page or a group.

What a group is for

Meta says groups provide "a space to communicate about shared interests with approved members", and suggests one for fans of your business or local enthusiasts with similar interests. As general advice, that makes a group a community its members shape as much as you do: posts that read as ads tend to land poorly, and someone has to approve members, answer questions and remove spam most days.

Meta's page on public and private groups says only current members can see posts in a private group, while anyone on or off Facebook can see what is shared in a public one. It gives a group of people who know each other, like a neighborhood, as an example of a private group.

When a group fits, and when it does not

  • It can fit when customers already want to talk to each other: pool owners swapping tips for a pool service, or homeowners in one area for a lawn care business.
  • It often does not fit one-off jobs, such as a roof repair, where customers have little reason to stay in touch with each other.
  • It rarely fits when nobody has time to moderate it every week; a quiet group with spam in it can look worse than no group.
  • If the aim is inquiries, a Page with messages turned on usually does that job without a group.

Joining local groups instead

Meta's page on public and private groups notes that a Page can join a group, and that all of that Page's admins can then see and interact with the group's posts and members. As general advice, read each group's rules before posting, since many limit self-promotion, answer questions helpfully, and mention your business only where the rules allow. The related answer on Nextdoor covers a similar neighborhood option.

Record an inquiry from a group post with social as its source channel, or referral when a member recommended you, and follow it to won, lost or still open.
