Will a new website or a new domain name hurt how you show up on Google?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

It can, for a while, and much of the risk is avoidable. Google says that changing the addresses of existing pages can cause ranking fluctuations, and that a medium-sized site can take a few weeks for Google to notice the change. A new domain changes every address at once. Keep the domain if you can, keep the pages that bring visitors, make sure every old address leads to its new page, and compare inquiries before and after.

Why a dip can happen

Google's guide to debugging drops in search traffic says that if you change the URLs of existing pages, "you may experience ranking fluctuations", and that, as a general rule, a medium-sized website can take a few weeks for Google to notice the change, with larger sites taking longer. Its starter guide adds that some changes might take effect in a few hours and others could take several months.

As general advice, the damage often comes from what a rebuild loses rather than from the design itself: pages that brought in visitors are dropped, old addresses lead to an error page, or forms stop working.

A new domain is the biggest change

Moving to a new domain changes the address of every page at once, and usually your email addresses too, as the related answer on how to choose a domain name notes. Google's starter guide says that, from a ranking perspective, keywords in a domain name alone "have hardly any effect", and advises doing what's best for your business when picking a name. As general advice, change domains for a business reason, such as a new name, not for search.

If you do change, register the new domain in the business's name, as the related answer on who should own your domain name explains, and, as general advice, keep the old one renewed so its addresses keep working.

What to insist the builder handles

You do not need the technical steps; ask for these outcomes in writing before work starts:

  • A list of every current page, and which new page replaces each one.
  • Every old address, including any printed on cards or vehicles, leads to the page that replaces it, not to an error page.
  • Pages that already bring in visitors keep their substance, as the related answer on whether to upgrade or rebuild your website advises.
  • Every form, phone number and booking link tested on launch day, with a test inquiry through each.
  • A check a few weeks after launch, and a plan if visits from search drop and do not recover.

Check inquiries before and after

Count inquiries by source for the same number of weeks before and after the launch date. Google's starter guide says you likely want to wait a few weeks to judge whether a change helped. If a drop after a move is not recovering, Google's traffic-drop guide points to common mistakes made when moving a site, so ask the builder to go through them. The related answer on how to tell if a website change worked covers the comparison.

If a new agency is building it

If the rebuild comes with a new agency, secure your domain, hosting and accounts in your own name first; the related answer on switching marketing agencies covers the order.
