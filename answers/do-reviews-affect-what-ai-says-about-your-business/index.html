Do online reviews affect what AI assistants say about your business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

They can. Google says it can use the words and phrases that repeat across a business's reviews to show its main themes on Maps, and in BrightLocal's 2026 survey 82% of consumers said they read AI-generated review summaries. A complaint that keeps coming back is the kind of theme a summary can carry, whatever the star average. The lasting fix is to fix its cause, then keep asking every customer for a review, with nothing offered in return.

What Google says it builds from reviews

Google's help page on business summaries in Maps says that when many reviews use similar words and phrases, data from them may be used to highlight parts of a business through Place Topics or review snippets. Place Topics show a business's main themes, are created only when it has enough customer reviews, and may display "a prevalent sentiment based on the sentiment of underlying reviews".

The same page says owners do not need to manage Place Topics, because they come from customers' reviews. If a topic is abusive, Google's advice is to flag the reviews behind it.

Where AI summaries come in

In BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, published February 11, 2026, 82% of consumers said they read AI-generated review summaries, and 23% were willing to rely on the summary alone. BrightLocal adds that summaries can hide details such as photos and how recent a review is, so the reviews behind a summary may stay influential longer.

Assistants such as ChatGPT are a different case. BrightLocal's survey says AI tools vary widely in how they source information. As general advice, from the outside it is usually hard to tell whether a given assistant read your reviews at all. Treat reviews as one input among several, not a lever.

Fix the cause, not the score

Because summaries pick up themes, a complaint that repeats, such as late arrivals, unreturned calls or a messy job site, is likely to keep showing up however many top ratings arrive around it. A higher average does not erase a pattern; fixing the pattern usually does, over time.

  • Read the last few months of reviews and write down any complaint that appears more than once.
  • Fix the cause in the work itself, then reply to the reviews that raised it, briefly and without arguing.
  • Keep asking every customer for a review, not only the happy ones, and offer nothing in return. The related answer on getting more reviews the right way covers how to ask.
  • Check again in a few months. The related answer on how Google calculates your star rating explains why the average moves slowly.

When the summary is simply wrong

Sometimes the problem is a wrong fact, not a real pattern. Google says it does not remove summaries "for being unclear or negative". It removes keywords and review snippets tied to an unrelated place, and editorial summaries that describe services the business does not offer. For mistakes in what assistants write, the related answer on whether AI assistants get facts wrong covers how to check and what you can correct.
