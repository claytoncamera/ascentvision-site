How do you automate the first steps with a new customer?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

Many steps between a customer saying yes and the first visit can run by themselves: a welcome message that says what happens next, a short intake form, the agreement sent for signature, a booking link, a deposit invoice if you take one, and reminders. Keep a person on the first call and on anything that needs judgment, send texts or automated calls only with the person's consent, and test every step as a customer would.

The steps that can run on their own

StepWhat it doesWorth checking
Welcome messageGoes out when the customer says yes; says what happens next, in order, and who to contactDoes it arrive the same day, from an address people recognize?
Intake formAsks only for what you need to startDo the answers land in the customer's record, with the source?
AgreementIs sent for signatureIs the signed copy saved where you can find it?
Booking linkLets the customer pick a time for the first visitDoes the visit appear on your calendar?
Deposit invoiceOnly if you take one; sent once the agreement is signedDoes a paid deposit show as paid?
RemindersGo out before the visit and for anything still missingDo they stop once the step is done?

What a person should still do

As general advice, keep these with a person:

  • The first call or visit, which lets you hear what the form missed.
  • Reading the intake answers before anything else is sent. Anything unusual or unclear goes to a person.
  • Anything that needs judgment: a change to the agreement, an unusual job, a customer who seems unsure.
  • Replies. If the customer answers the welcome message, answer by hand. The related answer on drip campaigns covers welcome sequences and stopping them when someone replies.

Texts and calls: consent first

Text or call a new customer only with the person's consent; the related answer on texting a new lead covers consent and stop requests. This is general advice, not legal advice.

Test it as the customer

  • Put a test customer through the whole sequence with your own email address and phone.
  • Check that the welcome message arrives and does not land in spam.
  • Fill in the form and look for the answers in the record.
  • Sign, book and, if you take a deposit, pay it, then check each result.
  • Each week, look for customers who said yes and stopped partway; that shows which step fails, and the test is worth repeating after any change.
