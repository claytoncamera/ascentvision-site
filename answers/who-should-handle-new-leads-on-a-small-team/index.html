Who should handle new leads when more than one person shares the work?

Ascent Vision · Published October 21, 2026

Give every new lead one named owner, picked by a simple rule written down in advance, and record the name where the whole team can see it. Name a backup for each person's days off, and have the owner leave a short handoff note whenever a lead changes hands. One owner and one note make it much less likely that anyone double-calls a lead or assumes someone else has it.

One named owner for every lead

The owner is the one person who replies, follows up and records its outcome as won, lost or still open. Set the owner when the inquiry arrives, before anyone replies, so it never sits in a shared inbox while everyone assumes a colleague has it. Adding an owner column to the sheet in the related answer on setting up a simple lead tracker is enough.

Choose the rule once, write it down and use it every time, so no one has to decide in the moment. Three simple ones:

  • Turns: leads go to each person in order, one after another.
  • Job type: whoever does that kind of work takes those leads.
  • Area: whoever covers that part of your service area takes them.

Check the name before you reach out

Before anyone calls, texts or emails a lead, they look at the owner column. If another name is there, they leave the lead alone. If it is empty, they fill in the owner by the written rule first, and that person replies. That quick check can stop two people contacting the same customer. Calls and texts go out only with the person's consent.

If a lead needs to move to someone else, change the name in the sheet and tell both people, so the record always matches who is really working it.

A backup for days off

Give each person one named backup and write the pairs down. Before a day off, the owner marks themselves away in the sheet, reviews their open leads and hands over anything waiting on a reply. Leads that arrive in the meantime go to the backup by the same rule.

It also helps to say that if an owner has not replied within the time you have told customers to expect, the backup takes the lead. The related answer on how fast to reply to a new lead covers choosing that time.

A short handoff note

Whenever a lead changes hands, the person passing it on leaves a note in the sheet. It can be brief, but it should say:

  • What the customer asked for, in their own words.
  • What has already been said, sent or agreed, such as a quote or a visit time.
  • Whether the customer has agreed to calls or texts, or prefers email.
  • The next step and the date it is due.
  • Whether the customer knows who will contact them next.

A weekly check

Once a week, one person scans the sheet for leads with no owner, an owner who is away, or no next-step date. Those are the gaps where leads often slip. If leads still slip with these rules in place, the related answer on CRMs covers when software is worth adding.
