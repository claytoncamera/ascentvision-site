How do you move your business phone number to a new provider without losing calls?

Ascent Vision · Published October 31, 2026

Start service with the new provider first, give it your number and account details, and do not cancel the old service until the number has moved. The FCC's consumer guide says FCC rules require simple ports to be processed in one business day, while wireline to wireless may still take a few days. Afterward, test calls in and out and check the greeting, voicemail, forwarding and call tracking. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

What the FCC's guide says

The FCC's guide to porting (reviewed December 10, 2025) is written for consumers, so ask your provider whether anything differs for a business account. It says not to terminate service with your existing company before initiating new service with another, and to contact the new company to start the process and give it your 10-digit number and any other information required.

It says your current contract may contain early termination fees or outstanding balances you are obligated to pay, but once you request service from the new company, the old company cannot refuse to port your number over an unpaid balance or termination fee, though you may still owe it. Companies may charge to port; you can ask whether fees can be waived.

On timing, it says FCC rules require simple ports, which generally do not involve more than one line or more complex adjustments to switching equipment, to be processed in one business day. Porting from wireline to wireless may still take a few days. A business moving several lines may want to ask whether its move counts as simple. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

When a number cannot move, or service changes

  • The guide says that if you are moving to a new geographic area, you may not be able to keep your current number.
  • Some rural wireline providers may obtain waivers from state authorities, so their customers may be unable to port; the guide points to your state public utilities commission.
  • Going from wireline to wireless, two phones may briefly share the number, and wireless 911 callback may be affected; the guide says to ask the new company first.
  • The guide says your wireline long distance company will not move with you, and that your new wireless company will likely provide long distance, which you should verify.

A business checklist (general advice)

  • Have ready: the account holder's name exactly as the current carrier has it, the number, the account number and any PIN, and the billing address.
  • Keep the new account in the business's name, with a login the owner controls.
  • Before the date, list everything tied to the number: greeting, voicemail, forwarding, call tracking numbers and any system that dials out from it.
  • After the move, test calls in and out, re-record the greeting, check voicemail and forwarding, and confirm call tracking still records.
  • Tell your team the date, so nobody cancels the old service halfway.
