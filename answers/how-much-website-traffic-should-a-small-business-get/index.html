How much website traffic should a small business get?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

There is no right number. A plumber serving one town and a national online shop need very different traffic, and a local site can do well on modest visits if the right people arrive. Compare your site with itself instead: this month against the same month last year, and inquiries per hundred visitors. Google Analytics can also show benchmarks against similar businesses, which are useful context but not a target.

Why no single number fits

A service business sells to the people in its area who need the service now, so its useful audience is small by design. Ten visits from people in your town looking for your service can be worth more than a thousand from elsewhere. Averages quoted online usually mix very different sites, so they say little about yours.

The comparisons that matter

  • This month against the same month last year, because most service work is seasonal.
  • The trend over several months, not day-to-day swings.
  • Inquiries per hundred visitors; the related answer on conversion rates covers how to work it out.
  • Visits to the pages that bring inquiries, such as service and contact pages, not the whole site.
  • Where visits came from; the related answer on organic, paid and referral traffic explains the labels.

What Google Analytics' benchmarks show

Google's Analytics Help says its benchmarking compares your property with a peer group of businesses in your industry category that also use Google Analytics, showing the median and the 25th to 75th percentile range next to your own trend line. Your default peer group comes from the industry you chose at setup and signals such as your site's address, and you can change it. For absolute numbers, Google scales the peers' figures to your own active users, to allow for differences in traffic volume; active users itself is compared with peers' actual counts.

It says benchmarking needs the "Modeling contributions & business insights" setting turned on, and that a peer group needs a minimum number of properties before benchmarks appear. As general advice, the categories are broad, so your peers may include businesses that sell nationally.

Read the number right

Google's help page on user metrics separates total users, everyone who visited, from active users, people who engaged, and says its reports show active users. It counts an engaged session as one that lasted 10 seconds or longer, had a conversion event, or had two or more page views. The related answer on what an owner should look at in Google Analytics covers the monthly view.

When traffic really is the problem

If inquiries per hundred visitors look healthy but visits are few, being found is often the problem to work on. If visits are steady but inquiries are not, the pages or the follow-up are the problem; the related answers on why a website gets no inquiries and on a website that gets traffic but no customers cover those.
