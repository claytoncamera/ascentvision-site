How do HVAC companies get leads?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

HVAC companies usually get leads from demand that follows the weather, so the work is to be easy to find and easy to choose when it arrives. That means Google Local Services Ads where eligible, a maintenance-plan page that works between seasons, manufacturer dealer-locator listings, and any lead company judged by booked jobs. Commercial work is a different buyer, often asking for bids rather than calls.

Demand follows the weather

Calls usually jump in the first heat wave and the first cold snap, and drop in between. The related answer on seasonal businesses suggests starting before the busy season and using quiet months for website and review work. For HVAC, a maintenance-plan page does that job: it gives someone with a working system a reason to get in touch, so say what a visit covers, how often it happens and how to sign up. The related answer on the marketing funnel notes that a local service business often has a short funnel, sometimes a single search and a call; a maintenance plan is the longer one.

Local Services Ads for HVAC

Google's page on getting started with Local Services Ads lists "HVAC (heating or air conditioning)" among its categories, says the ads are available only for certain categories, and points to an eligibility check for your area. One detail worth knowing for a new account: it describes pre-badge ads, which can run after preliminary checks while onboarding is finished. The related answer on what Google Local Services Ads are covers how the ads work, the screening and the per-lead billing.

Commercial leads are bids

A commercial buyer, such as a property manager, often compares written proposals rather than phoning the first result. A page for commercial work usually helps more than a call button alone: say which kinds of building you serve, how a request for a bid reaches you, what you need to see (plans, equipment list, building access) and how quickly you respond. Track these leads separately from repair calls.

Dealer-locator listings

Manufacturers publish pages that send homeowners to local installers. Trane's dealer locator opens with "Looking for a local HVAC expert? Your local Trane dealer can help." The page offers filters and badges, and those shown include Trane Comfort Specialist, NATE Certified and 24/7 Emergency Service. If you install a brand's equipment, check whether you appear on its locator and whether the details are current; the page links to a section for dealers and distributors.

Lead companies: what to ask

The related answer on lead-generation companies lists what to ask before paying per lead: who else gets each lead, what counts as billable, and whether the customer ends up dealing with you or with the seller. As general advice, also ask how the company collects each lead and how the person agreed to be contacted, and call or text them only with the person's consent. The related answer on lead attribution explains how to record where each inquiry came from and follow it to won, lost or still open.
