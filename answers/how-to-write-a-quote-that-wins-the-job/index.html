How do you write a quote that wins the job?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

A quote that tends to win the job reads like an answer to the customer's problem, not a price list. Restate what they need in their own words, say what is included and what is not, give the timing, explain what happens after they say yes, and offer one clear way to accept. Add a date the quote is good until only if there is a real reason for one, and never invent urgency.

What to put in it

PartWhat it says
The problemWhat they asked for, in their words, such as "water pooling by the back door after rain".
The workWhat you will do, in a few plain steps.
Included and not includedWhat the price covers, and what it leaves out, such as disposal or repairs found along the way.
The priceThe total, shown the way you normally price, with any options listed separately.
TimingWhen you can start and roughly how long the work takes.
What happens nextWhat follows a yes: booking, any preparation they need to do, and who will be there.
Good untilA date, only if the price really can change, for example when material costs move.
How to say yesOne step: reply yes, sign, or press one button.

Why their words matter

Customers often compare several quotes. One that repeats their problem back can show you listened, and a clearly spelled-out scope is easier to compare.

A short line on what is not included can prevent a common argument later. It can also make it easier for the customer to see when a cheaper quote simply leaves things out.

One clear way to say yes

A quote that ends with "let us know" leaves the next move unclear. One that ends with "reply yes to this email and we will send you start dates" does not. If they would rather talk it through first, offer a short call at a time they choose, and call only with the person's consent.

What to leave out

  • Invented urgency, such as a discount that supposedly ends tomorrow.
  • Pages of terms before the price. Put the plain summary first and the terms after it.
  • Jargon the customer would need to look up.
  • Vague lines such as "materials and sundries" with no explanation.

After you send it

Send it soon after the visit, while the job is fresh for both of you. If you hear nothing, follow up; the related answer on following up with leads that go quiet covers how often. If price comes up before you can quote, the related answer on when a lead asks for the price first covers that conversation.
