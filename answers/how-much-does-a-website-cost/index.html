How much does a website cost for a small business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 26, 2026

There is no single price. The cost depends on who builds it (you on a website builder, a freelancer or an agency), how many services and pages it covers, who writes the words and supplies the photos, whether it needs booking, quoting or payment features, and whether an existing site can be upgraded instead. Then come the running costs: the domain, hosting or a builder plan, and upkeep. A fair quote lists both and says what you will own.

What decides the build cost

  • Who does it: your own evenings on a builder, one freelancer, or a team. The related answer on building your own website or paying someone weighs the first choice.
  • Scope: a few standard services in one area is a smaller job than many services across a region.
  • Words and photos: who writes and supplies them. They are often the slow part, as the related answer on how long a website takes to build describes.
  • Features: a booking, quote or payment step, a Spanish version, or anything built around how you work.
  • Starting point: an existing site that can be upgraded usually costs less than starting over; the related answer on whether to upgrade or rebuild explains how to tell.

The running costs owners forget

  • The domain, renewed every term. ICANN's page on registrants' benefits and responsibilities says that if you choose automatic renewal, you must also keep your payment information current.
  • Hosting or a builder subscription, often with an introductory rate that changes at renewal; the related answer on what to look for in website hosting covers renewal terms.
  • Business email on your own domain, if the plan does not include it.
  • Paid add-ons, such as a premium theme, a booking tool or a form service.
  • Upkeep: updates, backups and testing that the forms still reach you, which the related answer on website maintenance lists.

Is a maintenance plan worth it?

Usually, when it covers the jobs you would otherwise skip: software updates, backups that can actually be restored, a monthly test of every form and phone link, and fixes within an agreed time when something breaks. It is usually weaker value when it is hosting resold with a vague promise of support. Ask what is done each month and how you will see it.

Comparing quotes

Ask every provider the same questions, in writing. The related answer on who owns a website a designer built explains why ownership belongs in the written agreement. Ascent Vision does not publish prices; every engagement is scoped on a call.

  • What is included, and what would be extra?
  • Who writes the words and supplies the photos?
  • What will it cost to run each year after launch, and for how long are any introductory rates fixed?
  • Will the domain, the site and every account be in the business's name?
  • What do I get back, and in what form, if we stop working together?
