Is sponsoring a local team, school or event worth it for a service business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

It can be, when the people at the games or the event are the people who hire you, and when you treat it as goodwill first and a lead source second. Get in writing what you receive, such as a banner, a program mention or a thank-you post, and give it a phone number or web address used nowhere else. Judge it by the inquiries it brings and the jobs they turn into, not by how visible it felt.

Goodwill first, leads second

Most local sponsorships work like brand awareness: people see your name next to something they care about, and some remember it later. That has value, but it is hard to see in a month's numbers; the related answer on brand awareness or leads covers the difference. Supporting your community is a fine reason on its own. Just decide up front whether you also need it to bring work, and set it up so you can tell.

A rule of thumb for saying yes

  • Say yes to the ones your customers attend. A youth league in the neighborhoods you serve often suits a home service business better than a large event across the region.
  • Prefer repeat exposure, such as a season-long banner at the field, over a single day.
  • Prefer something you can show up to. Being there in person often does more than a logo alone.
  • Turn down anything you would resent if it brought no work at all.

What to ask for

  • A written list of what you get: banner, jersey or program placement, mentions in emails or posts, and for how long.
  • A short web address or tagged link used only for this sponsorship. It is there to trace visits; the related answer on paying for links covers how Google treats links that come with a sponsorship.
  • A phone number used only for this sponsorship, if one is printed.

How to judge it

Record every inquiry that came through the banner, program or link under the campaign channel, with the team or event in the notes, and follow each to won, lost or still open. If a coach or parent recommends you to someone, that is a referral; note the sponsorship beside it. Before you renew, compare the jobs won with what you paid and the hours you gave. The related answers on whether a chamber of commerce is worth it and on getting referrals from other businesses cover two other local options.

A short tax note

Whether a sponsorship is deductible depends on whether it is advertising or a gift; the related answer on whether marketing is tax-deductible covers what the IRS says, and a tax preparer can tell you how it applies to you. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.
