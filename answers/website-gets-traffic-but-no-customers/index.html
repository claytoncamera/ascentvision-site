My website gets traffic but no customers. Who can fix that?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

It depends on where people drop out, so find that first. Look at three stages: visits that never become inquiries, inquiries that arrive but go cold, and inquiries that do not close. The first is the page and its ask, usually fixed by you or a web designer. The second is lead handling: slow replies, missed calls, after-hours gaps. The third is the offer, the price conversation and the fit, which usually rest with the owner.

Count the three stages first

For the last few months, write down visits, inquiries by any route (form, call, text or message) and customers won from them. Your website builder or host usually shows visits; the related answer on conversion rates covers that first step. Start where the drop is steepest. A number you cannot fill in is a leak of its own.

Stage one: visits that never become inquiries

This is the page and its ask. You can often fix the words yourself; a web designer or developer fixes speed, forms and layout. The related answers on why a website gets no inquiries and on calls to action cover the details. A fifteen-minute test:

  • See which pages get the visits. They often land on an inner page or an old post, not the homepage.
  • See where visits come from. If most are far outside your area or want something you do not sell, a better page will not help.
  • Then run the phone, speed and own-form checks in the related answer on why a website gets no inquiries; they apply here too.

Stage two: inquiries that arrive but go cold

In research reported in Harvard Business Review in 2011, firms that tried to contact a potential customer within an hour of receiving a query were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead, which the researchers defined as having a meaningful conversation with a key decision maker, as those that tried even an hour later. The related answer on how fast to reply to a new lead covers that research.

The fix is lead handling: an instant alert, a reply rule, an after-hours reply and follow-up, set up by you or by someone who builds lead systems. A fifteen-minute test:

  • For your last ten inquiries, note when each arrived and when someone first replied.
  • Count those that came after hours or mid-job, and those that never got a second message.
  • Send yourself an inquiry at eight in the evening and see when you would have answered.

Stage three: inquiries that do not close

This stage is usually the owner's to fix, since nobody outside knows your prices and the jobs you want; the related answer on why leads are not turning into customers covers the usual reasons and what to change. One cross-check belongs here: if many lost inquiries were never a fit, that is often a stage one problem, because the page is inviting the wrong jobs.

Sources

  1. James B. Oldroyd, Kristina McElheran and David Elkington, Harvard Business Review, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (March 2011)
