What do 'organic', 'paid' and 'referral' traffic mean in your website analytics?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

In Google Analytics, organic means visits from unpaid links, such as ordinary search results or links on social sites, paid means visits from ads, and referral means visits from links on other websites or apps. These are labels Google's rules give to visits, not to customers. Line them up with your own lead channels with care: a referral visit is a click from another site, while a referral lead is often a person who recommended you.

What Google's labels mean

Google Analytics sorts visits into default channels and describes each one on its help page. The ones owners ask about most:

ChannelGoogle's description, in short
Organic SearchNon-ad links in search results, which Google says include its AI Overviews and AI Mode
Organic SocialNon-ad links on social sites such as Facebook
Paid SearchAds on search engines such as Google
Paid SocialAds on social sites such as Facebook
ReferralNon-ad links on other sites or apps, such as blogs and news sites

Organic and paid in plain words

Organic does not mean natural or local. It means the visit came from a link that was not an ad. Paid means the visit came through an ad, on a search engine, a social site or elsewhere. Google says its channel definitions reflect its current view and may change as the market changes, and that the default channel group cannot be edited, so read the labels as Google's sorting rules rather than as facts about a person.

Why a referral visit is not a referral lead

In Google's report, Referral is a click on a link from another website or app. In your lead records, referral means another website or a person pointed the customer to you. A neighbor's recommendation leaves no link to click: the customer often searches your name or types your address, so the visit can show up as Organic Search or Direct. That is one reason word of mouth can be hard to see in analytics.

Links you share yourself work differently again. Google's rules sort a visit from a tagged link by the labels on it, so a link whose medium label says referral, for example, lands in Referral. In your lead records, log any tagged link you shared as campaign, as the related answer on what a tagged campaign link is describes.

What to do with the numbers

  • Ask every new inquiry how they found you, and record the channel as search, AI answer, social, referral, paid, email, campaign or direct.
  • Follow each lead to won, lost or still open.
  • Once a month, put visits by channel next to leads and jobs by channel, and watch the trend over months. A channel with many visits and few inquiries is worth a closer look.
