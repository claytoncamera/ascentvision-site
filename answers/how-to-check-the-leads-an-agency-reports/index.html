How do you check that the leads a marketing agency reports are real?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

Once a month, ask the agency for the list behind the count, not only the number, and match a sample against your own phone log, inbox and lead list. Reply to two or three of the people (by call or text only with their consent), look for duplicates, spam and people outside your area, and send a test through your own form. Then check that each reported lead has a source and an outcome. A mismatch is a reason to ask a question.

The monthly check

The related answer on what a marketing report should show says to ask for the list behind the numbers. This answer is what to do with it. As general advice:

  • Ask for the list: for each lead, the date, how the person got in touch, a name or number, the source and the outcome.
  • Match a sample from across the month against your own phone log, inbox and lead list. Each reported lead should trace to a real call, form or message you can find.
  • Reply to two or three about their own inquiry and ask how they found you. Call or text only with the person's consent.
  • Look for the same person twice, spam, and people outside the area you serve.
  • Send a test through your own contact form and see whether it reaches your lead list with a source. Tell the agency it is a test, so it is not counted.
  • Check that every reported lead has a source and an outcome of won, lost or still open.

What counts as a lead

Keep the definition from the related answer on the difference between a lead, a prospect and a qualified lead: a lead is someone who got in touch. A spam message is not a lead, and neither is a name on a bought list, which is a contact. The related answer on spotting spam and scam inquiries covers the signs.

What a mismatch may mean

A mismatch is a reason to ask, not proof of anything wrong; a different idea of what a lead is can be the whole cause. If answers stay vague month after month, treat that as information too.
