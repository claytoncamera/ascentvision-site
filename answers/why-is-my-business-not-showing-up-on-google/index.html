Why isn't my business showing up on Google?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Usually for a reason you can check. You may have searched your name, while customers search the service and the town. Your site may have no page that names that service and place in plain words. Your Google Business Profile may be missing, unverified or suspended. The site may lose the visitors it gets because it is slow or broken on a phone. Few people may search the words you expect. Or competitors may have more going for them. Check in that order, and beware anyone promising page one.

First, search the way a customer would

Your business name tells you little, since it is mostly typed by people who already know you. New customers usually type the service and the place, such as "roof repair in Tallahassee" or "dog groomer near me". Google's guide to how Search works says relevance can depend on things such as the searcher's location, language and device, so your results may differ from a customer's.

The checks, in order

  • No page for the service. Google first has to find a page and understand what it is about, and its guide says not every page makes it through each stage. A new site, or a service buried in a long list, gives it little to work with; the related answer on getting found for a new service covers what to do.
  • No working Business Profile. If you have none, or it is unverified or suspended, you may be missing from the map results that local searches often show; the related answers on what a Google Business Profile is and on why a profile gets suspended cover both cases.
  • Slow or broken on a phone. This rarely keeps a page out of Google by itself, but it loses the visitors who do arrive. Open your site on your phone, on mobile data; if it is slow, cut off or hard to tap, the related answers on whether a website needs to work on phones and on why a website gets no inquiries cover the fix.
  • Nobody searches those words. If customers say "AC repair" and your page only says "HVAC services", use their words. The words customers use when they call are the ones to put on the page.
  • Competitors have more going for them. If you appear, but below others, the related answer on why competitors show up on Google instead of you sets out a side-by-side check.

What to do this month

Google's own advice to a small local business is that "you can probably do much of the work yourself". A month is usually enough for the basics:

  • Write down five searches a customer would make, and run them on the same day each month.
  • Give each main service a clear place on your site, in your customers' words, with the area you serve.
  • Claim and verify your Business Profile, or fix whatever got it suspended.
  • Fix anything broken on a phone, starting with the phone number and the contact form.
  • Ask each new customer how they found you.

What not to pay for

Google's page on hiring an SEO says no one can promise a number one ranking on Google; as general advice, treat a promise of page one by a set date the same way. Google's guide to how Search works also says it does not accept payment to rank pages higher. A cold call or email saying your business is not on Google is usually a sales pitch; the related answer on how to spot an SEO scam covers it.
