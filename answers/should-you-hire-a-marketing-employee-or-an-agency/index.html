Should you hire a marketing employee, a fractional marketing lead or an agency?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

It depends on how much work there is, which skills it needs and who will manage it. An employee usually suits steady work that fills a week and that you can direct. A fractional marketing lead, an experienced marketer hired part time, often suits a business that needs a plan and oversight more than extra hands. An agency usually suits work that spans several skills. Whichever you choose, keep the domain, website, ad accounts and lead records in the business's name.

What a marketing hire is, and is not

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics describes advertising, promotions and marketing managers as people who plan programs to generate interest in products or services. It says they typically need a bachelor's degree and work experience in a related occupation, and that most work full time.

That is a planning role. A small service business often needs someone who also does the work: updating the website, posting, replying to inquiries and keeping the lead record. Those are different skills, so as general advice, list the weekly tasks before you write a job description, and hire for the tasks that are actually there.

Questions that usually decide it

  • Is there enough marketing work to fill most of a week, every week, or does it come in bursts?
  • Does the work need one skill or several, such as the website, search, ads and lead handling?
  • Who will manage the person or the team, and do you have that time?
  • If the person leaves or the contract ends, who holds the logins and the records?
  • How will you see what the work produced: every lead tagged with where it came from and followed to won, lost or still open?

Whichever you choose
