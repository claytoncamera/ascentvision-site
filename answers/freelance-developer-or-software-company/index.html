Should you hire a freelance developer or a software company?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

Either can build good software, so the better question is what the job needs. A freelance developer often suits a small, clearly described job where you want to deal with the person writing the code. A software company often suits a longer job, or software you will rely on every day, because other people can step in. Whichever you choose, ask who will understand the work after it ships and what the handover looks like.

The two routes side by side

Freelance developerSoftware company
Who does the workUsually one person, who plans, builds and testsUsually several people, each taking a part
If someone is ill, on vacation or leavesThe work may stop unless they have a backup they trustSomeone else can often step in, though they may need time to learn the project
Who runs the projectOften the developer, alongside the codingOften a project manager or lead, so you may talk to them rather than to the person coding
Who knows the work once it shipsOften one person, so written notes matter a great dealOften more than one person, if the company keeps good records
Skills on handOften strong in some areas; may bring others in for design or testingOften design, testing and security checks under one roof

How to decide

Yes to the first two questions and no to the third often points toward a freelancer. The reverse often points toward a company. Mixed answers are common, and a clear scope matters more than the label on either route.

  • Is the job small enough for one person to hold in their head?
  • Could your business wait a couple of weeks if that person were unavailable?
  • Will you rely on the software every day for years?
  • Does it need several skills, such as design, testing and security, or mostly programming?
  • Do you prefer one person to talk to, or a team with someone coordinating?

Questions to ask either one

Put the same questions to both. The related answer on who owns software built for your business covers the agreement, the related answer on what it takes to keep custom software running covers the care that follows launch, and the related answer on checking a developer's work covers approving it in stages.

  • Who else could read and change this work if you were unavailable for a few weeks?
  • Who tests it before I see it, and how will I see progress?
  • At the end, what do I receive: the code, a written description of how the pieces connect and how to set it up again, and a list of the services it relies on?
  • Whose name will the hosting, code storage and any app store accounts be in?
  • Who looks after it once it is live, and is that written down?

Starting with one, moving to the other

Some owners hire a freelancer for a first version and ask a second developer, or a company, to review it or take it over later. That is usually easier when the first developer keeps clear notes and the accounts are in your name from the start, so say so before work begins.
