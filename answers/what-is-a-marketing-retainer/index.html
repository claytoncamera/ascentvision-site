What is a marketing retainer, and how should one work?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

A marketing retainer is an agreement to pay a set monthly fee for an agreed amount of ongoing marketing work, instead of paying by the project or by the hour. A fair retainer spells out what gets done each month, how success is measured, how and when you get a report, how long the term is, how much notice either side must give, and that you own your accounts and the work.

How a retainer differs from other ways to pay

What you pay forSuits
RetainerOngoing work each monthWork that never really ends, like staying visible in search
ProjectOne defined result, like a new websiteWork with a clear finish line
HourlyTime actually spentSmall or unpredictable tasks

What a fair retainer spells out

In plain words: the work each month and what is not included, the few numbers you will both watch, how reporting works, the term and notice, and that your accounts and the work stay yours. The related answer on what a good SEO proposal includes gives the full checklist.

How one should work, month to month

A good retainer has a rhythm. You know what is planned for the month, the work gets done, and a report shows what changed and what is next. If the plan shifts, you hear why.

You should be able to ask what was done for your fee in any month and get a clear answer, not a list of vague activities.

Warning signs

Be wary of a vague scope, promises of a top spot or a fixed number of leads, accounts set up in the agency's name, a long term with no way out, and reports that never mention inquiries. The related answer on what SEO costs covers these in more detail.

Before you sign

Read the agreement in full and ask about anything unclear. Ask what happens on the day you leave: what you get back, how fast, and in what form. A fair agency answers that question easily. The related answer on switching agencies lists what to secure.
