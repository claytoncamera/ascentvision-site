Can an AI note-taker record your meetings and calls, and who has to agree?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

An AI note-taker can listen to a call or meeting, record it, write a transcript and summarize it. Because it records, treat it like any other recording: Florida generally requires everyone in the conversation to agree, so tell everyone at the start and get a yes before it runs. This is a plain summary, not legal advice. Keep the files private and check the summary before anyone acts on it.

What these tools do

An AI note-taker joins an online meeting or listens on a device, captures the audio, turns it into a written transcript and writes a summary with action items. Some keep the recording as well; others keep only the text. It is worth knowing which yours does, because either way the words of the meeting now exist in a file that someone can open.

Florida's recording law and meetings

The related answer on recording customer calls sets out the federal rule and Florida's section 934.03 for calls, so this page does not repeat it. One thing worth adding is the title of section 934.03, which refers to "wire, oral, or electronic communications", not only to phone calls. As general advice, treat a meeting, in a room or online, the way that answer treats a call.

Whether a particular tool, meeting or participant is covered is a question for an attorney, including when someone joins from another state. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Tell everyone, and get a yes

The related answer on recording customer calls has the plain line to ask and what to do if someone says no. What a note-taker adds:

  • Put it in the meeting invite, so people know before they join.
  • Some tools join as a named participant, which helps people see that it is there. Check whether yours does.
  • Tell your own staff too. A note-taker running without the people in the meeting knowing can cost trust, whatever the law requires.

Where it goes and who can read it

The recording and transcript usually sit with the tool's company, and a summary may be emailed to people automatically, so check who receives it by default. The related answer on what happens to what customers tell your chatbot covers where AI-tool records go, who can read them and how long they are kept.

When to leave it off

As general advice, leave the tool off for meetings about a customer's private details, pay, reviews, discipline, health or legal advice. The related answer on putting customer information into ChatGPT lists what usually should not go into an outside tool.

Check the summary before it is acted on

A summary can leave out a decision, swap a name or a number, or credit the wrong person. As general advice, read it against the transcript for names, dates, amounts and promises before anyone acts on it, and keep the transcript until you have. Do not forward an unchecked summary to a customer; the related answer on whether a person should approve what AI sends to customers covers that.

Sources

  1. Florida Statutes (2026), section 934.03, "Interception and disclosure of wire, oral, or electronic communications prohibited"
