Do mentions on other websites help you show up in AI answers?

Ascent Vision · Published October 31, 2026

They can help, but no one can promise it. Assistants that search the web often draw on what is published about a business, including pages other people write, and no outsider can see how much any one mention counts. Honest mentions a local business can earn include local news, trade and community groups, suppliers and partners, and a chamber. Quietly paid mentions are best avoided.

What assistants say they draw on

Two assistants' help pages describe using the web. Microsoft's FAQ for Copilot Chat at work says it "leverages the latest information from the web" and Anthropic's help page tells users to review Claude's cited sources when it uses web search results. So pages other people write about a business can be part of what an assistant reads. The related answer on why ChatGPT recommends a competitor covers the differences an owner can see.

Perplexity's help page on source labels says it "rates the whole website a source comes from", using checks such as whether the site corrects its mistakes and says who wrote each piece. It also says its payments and other business arrangements do not affect a site's label. That page is about labels on cited sources, not about how Perplexity picks which sources to cite.

Honest mentions a local business can earn

  • Local news: a story about an opening, an anniversary, an event or community work. Offer facts, not payment.
  • Trade and community groups: a members page that names your business, in groups you actually belong to.
  • Suppliers and partners: a page that lists you as an installer, dealer or partner, when they agree.
  • A chamber of commerce: a member directory entry, if you belong.
  • Whatever the source, ask for the facts to be right: your full name, town and what you do. Social media and YouTube posts are public mentions too, so keep the facts in them correct.

What to avoid

Paid links meant to lift rankings. Google's spam policies list "Buying or selling links for ranking purposes" as an example of link spam. The related answer on paying for links to your website covers it.

Paid mentions that read as independent opinion. In its endorsement guidance, the FTC says an endorser's connection to a business that a significant minority of consumers would not expect, and that would affect how they weigh the endorsement, should be "disclosed clearly and conspicuously". The page says the same usually applies when the endorser has been paid or given something of value. If you pay for a mention, a good start is to ask that it be labeled plainly, or not to pay. This is the agency's guidance on endorsements, and whether a particular payment or mention counts is a question for a lawyer. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Posting about your own business on forums has its own risks; the related answer on Reddit and forums covers them.

How to see which mentions an assistant is drawing on

Ask the questions your customers would ask and open the sources the answer shows, as the related answer on getting ChatGPT to recommend your business describes; a page that keeps appearing and describes you correctly is worth keeping accurate, and one with a wrong detail is something to fix. Not every assistant shows sources, and no one can promise that a mention will lead to a recommendation.
