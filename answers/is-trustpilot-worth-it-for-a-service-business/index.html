Is Trustpilot worth it for a local service business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Usually not as a first priority. Trustpilot lets a business claim its profile, reply to reviews and invite customers for free, and sells paid plans with extra tools. It tends to suit businesses that sell online; customers of local service businesses usually read Google reviews first. Claim your profile if customers already review you there, invite everyone the same way with no incentives, and pay for a plan only if your records show customers using it.

How Trustpilot works

Trustpilot's Trust Centre says consumers decide which businesses are reviewed and Trustpilot hosts the reviews. Anyone can write one about a genuine experience, not only a purchase, and businesses "can never pay to remove reviews". Its guidelines say that once someone reviews a business, a profile page is created and stays visible even if never claimed, so you may already have one.

The guidelines say people over 18 with a recent, genuine experience can review you, and that an experience can be a phone call or an email.

Free and paid

Trustpilot says its basic services are free for businesses, including reading, replying to, inviting and reporting reviews, and that it earns revenue from businesses that subscribe to additional software. A claimed profile shows the label "Claimed" and gets the free plan's features; paid plans add more advanced features such as AI tools or custom widgets. Its plans page says all contracts are a 12-month commitment, so read the terms first.

Who it suits

As general advice, Trustpilot fits best where customers buy from a website: its plans page prices each website domain separately and lists eCommerce integrations. A local plumber or cleaner is usually judged on Google first. In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, Google's share as a source of reviews fell from 83% in 2025 to 71% but stayed far ahead; Trustpilot was among the sites whose use grew.

The related answer on Google or Yelp reviews explains where to send customers; Trustpilot rarely changes that.

Trustpilot's rules on asking for reviews

Trustpilot encourages invitations but says "We don’t allow incentivized reviews on Trustpilot". Its guidelines ask businesses to:

  • Invite everyone the same way, at the same point in the customer journey, whether their experience was good or bad.
  • Use neutral wording, and never suggest that only positive reviews are wanted.
  • Offer nothing for a review: its examples include discounts, promo codes, prize draw entries, refunds and freebies.
  • Invite all customers, not only happy ones, and never steer unhappy ones to contact you instead of reviewing.
  • Keep review collection off business computers, tablets and phones.
  • Leave out family, staff and anyone else with a special relationship to the business.

How to decide

Trustpilot says misuse can lead to a Consumer Warning on your profile; the related answer on getting more reviews the right way covers Google's and the FTC's rules on the same points. If customers already review you on Trustpilot, claim the free profile and reply to reviews. Ask new customers how they found you and which reviews they read; the related answer on the options for 'How did you hear about us?' covers the wording. Pay for a plan only if Trustpilot keeps coming up, and judge it as the related answer on paid directory listings suggests: by the inquiries and jobs it brings.
