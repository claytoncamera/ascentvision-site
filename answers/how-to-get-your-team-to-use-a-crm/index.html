How do you get your team to actually use the CRM?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

A CRM tends to go unused when entering data is extra work and nobody looks at what was entered. What usually helps: ask for only a handful of fields, let connected tools enter what they can, look at open leads together for a few minutes each week, keep one place for every inquiry, name an owner and drop whatever nobody uses. A habit that fits the day often beats a tool with more features.

Why a CRM goes unused

These reasons are common, not universal. Ask your own team which one applies.

  • Too many fields, so each entry feels like filling in a form.
  • Entry is extra work on top of the job, often left until the end of a long day.
  • Nobody looks at it, so nobody sees a reason to keep it up.
  • Phones and inboxes stay the real record, because that is where the conversation happens.

What tends to help

  • A handful of required fields. Require only what the weekly look uses: the core fields the related answer on what a CRM is lists, including the outcome (won, lost or still open). Make the rest optional.
  • Entries made by the connections. When a web form or a message creates the record itself, people only add the next step. The related answer on which business tools to connect covers the pairs that often earn it, and a weekly count that shows when a connection has stopped. If a connection sends texts or reminders to customers, they go out only with the person's consent.
  • A short weekly look at open leads, together. Go through what is open, who owns it and what happens next. A simple rule is that an inquiry not in the CRM does not get discussed.
  • One place for every inquiry. Calls, emails, messages and walk-ins all end up in the same list, even when they arrive in different inboxes.
  • A named owner. One person looks after the list and each lead has one owner. The related answer on who should handle new leads covers choosing the rule, and the related answer on cleaning up your customer list covers the data.
  • Dropping what nobody uses. After a month, ask which fields and steps people skipped, and remove or merge them.

A first month

As general advice, one way to sequence it:

WeekWhat to do
OneCut the required fields to the handful. Give everyone who answers inquiries their own login with only the access their job needs; the related answer on who should have access to your CRM covers how.
TwoConnect the form and any other source that can create a record itself, and send a test inquiry through each.
ThreeHold the first weekly look, with the open leads on screen.
FourAsk what people skipped or worked around, and drop it.

If it still does not stick

If people keep notes elsewhere after a month, the tool may be the wrong fit rather than the team.
