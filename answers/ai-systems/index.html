Answers about AI automation for business

What repetitive work AI can take on in a small business, what it should never be left alone with, and how to keep an automated system honest.

  • What can a small business realistically automate with AI?

    A plain map of the tasks a small business can hand to AI and automation, such as routing and reminders, and the ones that still need a person's judgment.

  • What should you ask before hiring someone to build AI for your business?

    Questions to put to anyone who offers to build AI for your business: ownership, where your data goes, human review, the off switch, testing and upkeep.

  • How do you tell if a task is worth automating?

    A six-question test for any task: how often it repeats, how many steps, how fixed they are, what a mistake costs, who approves and how you would notice.

    Keep a person reviewing AI messages at the start and on anything risky. See when relaxing review is reasonable, what to check first and what to log.

    An AI system can often connect to email, calendar, forms, spreadsheets, CRM, accounting and job software. Check your plan, logins, data and off switch.

    Published studies find real but uneven time savings. See what two of them measured, what they did not, and how to measure your own week before and after.

  • What is the difference between automation and AI?

    Automation follows fixed rules you set; AI interprets wording and drafts. See when each is cheaper, safer and easier to check, and how they combine.

    AI can draft a first version from your price rules, past jobs and scope notes. A person should decide the final price, unusual jobs and every promise.
