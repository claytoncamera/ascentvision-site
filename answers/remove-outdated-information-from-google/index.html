How do you get outdated or wrong information about your business off Google Search?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Start with the site that published it, because Google does not own other websites. Google's help says to contact the website owner, and that even when Google drops a result, the page itself is still there. Once the site's owner removes the old information, Google says it eventually leaves its results through regular updating, and Google offers a request to refresh an outdated result. Wrong details on your own Google Business Profile are a separate fix.

Where the fix starts

Google's page on contacting a website owner says that to remove something from a website, you usually need to talk to its owner: "Even if you found it on Google, Google doesn’t own the website." Even if Google takes a page off its results, it says, people can still reach the page by its address, through social media or from other search engines.

Its suggestions include a "Contact us" link or email address on the site and, if the owner cannot be reached, the company that hosts it. As general advice, ask for one specific change, such as an old phone number or a closed location, and give the correct detail.

Asking Google to refresh an old result

Google's page on refreshing outdated content says that if content was deleted from a site but still comes up in Google Search, the page description or cache might be outdated. Its refresh request asks for the address of that page, and a separate request covers an outdated image. If you have already updated a live page, the page says you "don't have to fill out the form".

Google's page on removing personal content adds that once a site's owner removes information, it will eventually leave Google Search as part of Google's regular updating process. As general advice, fix the source first: the request is for a result that no longer reflects the page.

What Google will not do

Google's pages describe refreshing or removing its own results, not editing someone else's page. Its page on contacting a website owner says information that shows up on Google is on the internet, and adds: "It doesn’t mean Google agrees with it." As general advice, a page that is still live and still wrong usually stays in the results until its owner changes it. Old addresses left by your own rebuild are covered in the related answer on whether a new website or domain hurts your Google results.
