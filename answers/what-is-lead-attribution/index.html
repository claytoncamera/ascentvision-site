What is lead attribution, and why does a small business need it?

Ascent Vision · Published October 14, 2026

Lead attribution means recording where each inquiry came from, such as a Google search, an AI answer, social media, a referral, an ad or an email, and following it to an outcome. For a small business it is the difference between knowing which marketing brings in customers and guessing.

Why it matters

Without attribution, every channel looks equally good or equally bad, and spending decisions are made on feel. With it, an owner can see that, say, most new customers came from search and few from a paid campaign, and move money accordingly. It also settles who gets credit when an agency is paid on results.

The channels worth tracking

  • Search: someone found you on Google or another search engine.
  • AI answer: an assistant such as ChatGPT or Perplexity sent them.
  • Social: a post, profile or message on a social network.
  • Referral: another website or a person pointed them to you.
  • Paid: an ad you paid for.
  • Email: a message you sent.
  • Campaign: a tagged link you shared, such as one in a flyer or a bio.
  • Direct: they typed your address or came back on their own.

How to start

Put every inquiry in one place, whatever route it arrives by. Record the source at the moment the inquiry arrives, not weeks later from memory. Record what happened: won, lost or still open. Review it monthly. Even a simple version of this beats an expensive dashboard that nobody connects to actual customers.

Where it gets hard

Customers often touch several channels before they get in touch, so a single source is a simplification. A useful rule is to keep both the first way someone found you and the last thing that brought them back, and to be consistent about which one you report.
