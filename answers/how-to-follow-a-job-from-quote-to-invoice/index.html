How do you follow a job from quote to invoice, and do you need job management software?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

Keep one record for each job and move it through named stages: quote sent, accepted, scheduled, done, invoiced and paid. Give each stage the few facts it needs and one person who moves it on, and keep changes on the same record so the quote and the invoice can be compared. Three questions show whether a shared sheet is enough or job software would help.

One record, named stages

The related answer on a sales pipeline covers the stages up to a win. This page picks up at the quote and follows the job to the money. As general advice, each stage should mark something that actually took place, with the facts below written down.

StageFacts to recordWho moves it on
Quote sentJob address, scope in writing, price, date sentWhoever wrote the quote
AcceptedDate and how they said yes, any deposit, agreed start windowWhoever took the answer
ScheduledDate, who is going, materials neededWhoever books the work
DoneWhat was actually done, any changes and who agreed themThe person who did the work
InvoicedInvoice sent date and due dateWhoever bills
PaidDate and amount receivedWhoever checks payments

Keep the quote and the invoice on one record

When the quote lives on the same record as the job, the accepted lines can become the invoice without retyping, and every change agreed on site is added to that record. The price agreed and the price billed are then easy to compare, which can help if a customer questions the bill. The related answer on sending invoices and reminders covers what to automate once a job is invoiced.

Where jobs usually get stuck

Check the three groups below once a week, oldest first; it can catch jobs that have slipped.

  • Done but not invoiced: work finished and nobody billed it.
  • Invoiced but not paid: a reminder is due.
  • Accepted but not scheduled: a customer said yes and is waiting.

Do you need job management software?

A sheet is often enough while jobs stay on track.

The first question is the one to start with: do jobs or invoices slip even when the sheet is kept up? Two or more yes answers, counting the questions in the related answer on field service software about several people sharing the schedule and retyping details, suggest software may earn its place.

If so, the related answer on field service software compares two products and what to check before signing, and the related answer on which business tools are worth connecting covers joining tools you already have. Whatever you pick, check that you can export your jobs and that the account is in the business's name.
