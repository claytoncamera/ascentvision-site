Should a small business use an AI chatbot or live chat with a person?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

Live chat is a person answering in real time. An AI chatbot is an assistant answering from facts you approved. Live chat needs someone free to answer during the hours it shows as open. An assistant needs checking and a hand-off to a person. A common mix is the assistant out of hours and a person during hours. If neither beats a form and a fast reply, skip both.

The common mix

A common mix is the assistant out of hours and a person during hours. The hand-off depends on the hour. In hours, a person takes over when the assistant is unsure or the visitor asks. Out of hours, the assistant says when a person will reply. Promise only a time you can keep; the related answer on after-hours inquiries covers it.

Either way, each inquiry should reach your lead list with its source, not stay inside the chat.

Questions that decide it

  • Is anyone free to answer during the hours the chat shows? A chat that says online and goes unanswered can be worse than having none.
  • The related answer on whether to use a chatbot at all covers the rest: repeat questions, visitors out of hours and who reads the conversations.

When neither beats a form and a fast reply

If few visitors would use chat, nobody can watch it, or most questions need judgment, a short form and a fast reply may do the job. The related answer on whether to use a chatbot at all covers when a form and a fast reply are often enough and what to settle before adding a chatbot. The related answer on stopping an AI assistant from making things up covers testing one. Call or text a visitor back only with the person's consent.
