How can a small business send invoices and payment reminders automatically?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

A small business can usually let its software send an invoice when a job is marked done, ask for a deposit when a quote is accepted, and send reminders before and after the due date. A person should still check unusual jobs, credits and disputes, and reminders should stop as soon as a payment is recorded. Texts go out only with the person's consent. Keep a record of what was sent and when it was paid.

What can run on its own

  • An invoice when a job is marked done, built from the quote or agreed price.
  • A deposit request when a customer accepts a quote or books a date.
  • A reminder a few days before the due date, one on the day and one or two after it.
  • A receipt once the payment arrives.

What a person should still check

As general advice, keep a person on anything that is not routine: a job whose price changed on site, a credit or refund, a customer who has said they dispute the bill, and the first few invoices from any new setup. Before the first one goes out, check that it names the right customer, that the amount matches the quote or agreement, and that the payment is recorded where you keep your books. If an AI system drafts the messages, the related answer on whether a person should approve what AI sends covers when review can be relaxed.

Tone, timing and stopping

A first reminder can be short and friendly: the invoice number, the amount, the due date, how to pay and who to contact if something looks wrong. Later ones can be firmer but should stay polite. As general advice, avoid adding a late fee that your written agreement or quote does not already state, and ask your accountant or attorney what applies to you.

Reminders should stop as soon as a payment is recorded, so test that a part payment, a refund and a payment by check all show correctly; the related answer on which business tools to connect lists the part-payment and refund check for invoicing and payments. Pause reminders for any customer who has raised a dispute.

Email, texts and getting paid

Email reminders are usually the simple part, though anyone who asks to stop getting them should be taken off. Texts are different: send them only with the person's consent, and give a plain way to stop them. The related answer on texting a new lead or a past customer covers consent and opting out. The related answer on fake invoices and payment-change emails suggests telling customers how you take payment, and the related answer on choosing a payment processor covers the account the money lands in.

Keep a record

Log each invoice, each reminder and the date it was paid. A paid deposit is the clearest sign a lead was won, as the related answer on taking payments on a website puts it, so mark that lead won and keep its source. Ask your accountant which records to keep and for how long. If you change tools, turn off the old tool's automatic messages so customers do not get two; the related answer on switching business software covers the order.
