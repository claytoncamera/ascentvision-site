How should a business respond to a negative Google review?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Reply briefly and calmly, for the customers who read it later. Google's tips: be polite, apologize when appropriate, admit real mistakes but not things outside your control, never share the reviewer's private information, and, when it is complex, offer to talk by phone or email. The FTC says a business may reply publicly, but its rule bars knowingly or recklessly false accusations about a reviewer, and threats or intimidation to get a review removed or changed. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Who reads your reply

Google says an approved reply is posted publicly under the review as the business, not under your name; the reviewer is notified and can still change the review. Google checks replies first, usually within 10 minutes but sometimes up to 30 days, and you must verify the business before you can reply; the related answer on how to verify a Google Business Profile covers that.

As general advice, write for the next customer. An old review with no reply can still be read, so a short reply now usually beats none, though Google favors replying promptly. Asking for reviews is covered in the related answer on getting more reviews the right way.

A reply to a fair complaint

Google's tips suggest a reusable shape; the third step is general advice:

  • Use their name and their specific point, as Google suggests, and sign off with your name or initials.
  • Apologize if you got it wrong: Google says a genuine apology can go a long way in rebuilding trust.
  • Say what you changed or will do: one concrete step usually reads better than a promise.
  • Take the details offline: Google suggests politely asking the reviewer to contact you by phone or email when a situation is complex.

A reply to an unfair or mistaken one

Google's tips say not to take responsibility for things outside your control, and to explain what you can and cannot do. As general advice, state the facts once, with no sarcasm; if you cannot find the person in your records, say so politely and invite them to call you.

If the review breaks Google's rules, reporting it is a separate step; the related answer on getting a fake Google review removed covers that.

What to keep out of a reply

Google's tips say: "Never share a reviewer's private information or engage in personal attacks". As general advice, that usually means leaving out the job, the address and the bill, even if the reviewer raised them.

Google also says not to use replies to offer deals or promotions, and it strictly prohibits offering incentives, such as discounted goods or services, in exchange for changing a review or removing a negative one.

Where the FTC draws the line

The FTC's questions-and-answers page on its consumer reviews rule says a business can respond publicly to a negative review, but "you should watch what you say"; the related answer on getting a fake Google review removed covers what the rule bars. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.
