Why are my leads low quality, and how do you get better ones?

Ascent Vision · Published October 26, 2026

Low-quality leads usually come from the source, not bad luck: marketing that reaches people outside your area or services, an offer that draws price shoppers, a form so easy that the barely interested send it, or a report that counts clicks as leads. Find which source sends them from your own lead records, then tighten that one source: who it reaches, what it promises and what it asks before someone gets in touch.

Define a good lead first

Write down what a good lead is for you: someone who needs a service you offer, in an area you serve, on a timeline you can meet. The related answer on telling whether a lead is a good fit lists the questions. Tag every lead with its source and whether it fit. After a month, the sources with the most wrong-fit leads usually stand out.

If the leads fit but still do not become customers, the leak is after they arrive; the related answer on why leads are not turning into customers covers that side. This answer is about the source.

Causes and fixes

CauseWhat it looks likeFix to try
ReachInquiries from outside your area, or for work you do not doName your services and area plainly in the ad or page, and narrow where the ads run
PromiseMost inquiries ask only about the discountLead with the work and the area; keep any offer secondary
FrictionMany forms, few real conversationsAdd one fit question, such as the town or the service needed
CountingA report shows far more leads than your listCount only people who actually got in touch
Shared leadsSeveral businesses call the same personJudge the lead site by jobs won from it

Search ads that reach the wrong people

Google Ads Help says negative keywords "let you exclude search terms" from your campaigns. Its example is an optometrist who sells eyeglasses excluding searches such as "wine glasses". As general advice, look at what people actually typed before they reached you, and exclude the searches for work you never do.

Forms, lead sites and reports

A quick one-tap form tends to bring more forms and more people who were only mildly interested; the related answer on Facebook lead ads describes Meta's More volume and Higher intent form types. Leads a site shares with several businesses turn every inquiry into a race to reply, as the related answer on buying leads from lead sites covers, and a report that counts taps or page views as leads makes every source look busier than it is; see the related answer on what counts as a conversion in Google Ads.

Fewer, better leads can be the win

A change that cuts inquiries but raises jobs won is working. Judge each source by jobs won and the cost of each job won, not lead count; the related answer on what CAC, ROI and ROAS mean shows how to work out cost per job won. Change one thing at a time and compare a full month with the one before.
