How does an AI system connect to the tools you already use?

Ascent Vision · Published October 28, 2026

Usually by you giving it permission to use an account you already have. Email, calendars, forms, spreadsheets, customer records, accounting and job software can often be connected, but it depends on the tool and your plan. Before you agree, check that your plan allows outside connections, that the account is in your name, what information goes where, and how you switch the connection off yourself.

Which tools can usually be connected

Many tools in these groups offer a way to connect, though what is allowed varies by product and by plan. Some older or very small tools offer no connection at all, so ask before assuming.

ToolWhat an AI system might do with itWorth asking
EmailSort messages and draft replies for you to approveCan it only read, or can it also send?
CalendarCheck free times and propose bookingsWhose calendar, and can it change entries?
Web formsReceive new inquiries and pass them onDoes every form reach it?
SpreadsheetsRead a price list or add rows to a lead listWhich sheets can it see?
CRMLook up or update customer recordsCan it edit or delete records?
AccountingRead invoices and balances for a reportIs view-only access available?
Job softwareLook up jobs, schedules and statusDoes your plan allow outside connections?

Four things to check before you connect it

  • Does your plan allow it? Some tools limit outside connections to certain plans or can charge extra for them. The tool's help pages or its support team can tell you.
  • Who owns the login? Use an account in your name.
  • What can it reach? Ask for the narrowest access that does the job, such as view-only on the accounting tool.
  • Can you switch it off yourself? Check that you can disconnect it in the tool's own settings.

Start small and keep a list

Try the connection on a copy or a small part first, such as one calendar or a test spreadsheet, and widen it one step at a time. Read-only access is a sensible place to begin.

Keep a short list of every connection: the tool, whose login it uses, what it can do and the date it was connected. When you want to switch something off, or change who works for you, the list shows you what to disconnect.
