How long does it take to build or upgrade a small business website?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

How long a small business website takes depends less on the building than on how quickly words, photos and decisions arrive. Upgrading a site that already works is often a matter of a few weeks; a new site with many pages can take longer. Projects most often stall while waiting for the owner's content and approvals, so gathering those before work starts is usually the biggest single way to speed things up.

What decides the timeline

  • Upgrade or new: fixing a site that works is usually quicker than starting again.
  • Size: a handful of pages moves faster than a page for every service and every town.
  • Content: whether the words, photos and service details are ready, or still need to be written and taken.
  • Decisions: how many people have a say, and how quickly they agree.
  • Approvals: how many rounds of changes each page goes through.
  • Access: whether the logins for the domain, hosting and current site are easy to find.

Where projects stall

The most common delay is waiting on words and photos. Owners are busy running the business, and writing a page about it can slip down the list for weeks. Photos of real jobs and people often need a day set aside, and sometimes good weather.

Other common stalls: feedback that arrives from several people with different views, new ideas added late, and a login nobody can find. Each one can pause the work even when the building itself is done.

Upgrade or new build

UpgradeNew build
What changesSpecific pages, speed on a phone, wording, the contact pathMost or all of the site
What you provideCorrections and approvalsMost of the content, photos and decisions
Usual paceOften a few weeksOften longer, and mostly set by how fast content arrives

How to speed it up

  • Name one person who makes the final call.
  • Before work starts, list your services, the areas you cover and what makes you different.
  • Write rough answers in your own words; someone else can tidy them later.
  • Gather real photos of your team and finished jobs early.
  • Find the logins for your domain and hosting on day one.
  • Agree on a launch date and on when feedback is due.
  • Launch the core pages first and add the rest later.

What not to rush

Before launch, send a test inquiry through every form and tap every phone number on a phone. A site that goes live a week later but delivers every inquiry is usually worth more than one that goes live on time with a broken form.
