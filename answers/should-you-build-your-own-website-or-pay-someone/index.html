Should you build your own website or pay someone to build it?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Building it yourself on a website builder can be enough when you offer a handful of services, can write plainly about them and have the hours to spare. Paying someone usually makes sense when your time earns more on paid work, the site has to do more than describe you, or a first attempt has stalled. Either way, the site should be clear on a phone, get every inquiry to a person, and sit on a domain your business owns.

What a builder handles well

Website builders usually give you a tidy template that adapts to phones, run the hosting and updates for you, and let you edit text and photos yourself. For a business with a few standard services in a set area, that can be a perfectly good site. The related answer on whether a website template is good enough compares templates with custom design; this answer is about who does the work.

The parts owners often miss

Clear words, speed on a phone and real photos matter more than the design, as the related answer on whether a template is good enough explains. A do-it-yourself site more often slips on the parts nobody sees:

  • An inquiry path that reaches a person: send a test through every form, tap every phone number, and check the messages land in an inbox someone reads every day.
  • A way to see where inquiries come from, such as a "How did you hear about us?" question on the form.
  • Ownership: the domain and the builder account in your business's name, not in a helper's personal account.
  • Renewals: the plan and the domain renewing on a card someone watches, with notices going to an inbox more than one person reads.
  • A second person who can log in, so the site does not depend on whoever built it.

Where the hours go

Putting the pages together is rarely the slow part. The time goes into the words, the photos and the decisions, the same things that hold up paid projects, as the related answer on how long a website takes to build describes. Be honest about how many evenings that will take, and weigh them against what those hours would earn on paid work.

When paying someone makes sense

  • A first attempt has sat half-finished for months.
  • The site has to do more than describe you, such as a booking or quote step built around how you actually work.
  • You want someone accountable for testing that every form and call reaches you, and for fixing it when it breaks.
  • You already have a site that brings few inquiries. Upgrading it is often the first thing to try; the related answer on whether to upgrade or rebuild explains how to tell.

If you do pay someone

Keep the same ownership rules: the domain and every account in your business's name, with the designer added as a user. The related answer on what to ask before hiring a web designer lists the questions to settle in writing before work starts.
