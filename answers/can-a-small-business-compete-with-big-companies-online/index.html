Can a small business compete with big companies on Google and in AI answers?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

Often, yes, but not everywhere. A small business usually cannot outspend large companies on broad, national searches. It can compete where it is more specific than they are: a particular service, a particular kind of customer, a particular area, and answers that show real first-hand experience. Choosing that ground is most of the work.

Where small businesses can win

  • Specific services: "emergency boiler repair" rather than "heating".
  • Specific customers: "bookkeeping for restaurants" rather than "accounting".
  • Specific places, where you genuinely serve customers.
  • First-hand answers: what you have actually seen and done, which a large company's generic page cannot match.
  • Speed: answering an inquiry in minutes, which many larger firms do not.

Where it is usually hard

  • Broad, national terms that large companies already dominate.
  • Price comparisons, where scale wins.
  • Anything that needs a big budget to show up at all, such as competitive ad auctions.

How to choose your ground

List the services you most want more of and the customers you serve best. For each, ask: who else is visible when a customer searches or asks an assistant? Start where the competition is thinnest and your experience is deepest, then widen.
