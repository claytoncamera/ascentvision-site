How does a service business get more repeat customers?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

Make the next job easy to say yes to. Keep a record of what you did for each customer, remind them when the work is usually due again, keep booking just as simple each time, and thank people after every job. A maintenance plan or membership can help if its renewal terms are plain and leaving is easy. Record each returning customer's new job, so you can see how much work comes back.

The habits that bring customers back

Most of these cost little once they are a habit; the related answer on email marketing covers what to send and how often.

  • A record of each job: what you did, what you noticed and when it will likely be due again.
  • A reminder when the work is usually due, such as a seasonal service or a yearly check, sent by email, or by text or call only with the person's consent.
  • The same easy way to book every time, whether that is a phone number, a reply to your email or a booking link.
  • A thank-you after the job, with one plain way to reach you next time.

Reminder emails follow the commercial email rules

The Federal Trade Commission's CAN-SPAM compliance guide gives a message to former customers announcing a new product line as an example of email that must comply with the law. Whether a given reminder counts as commercial depends on its primary purpose, and the guide says the law views the transactional or relationship categories narrowly. The related answer on email marketing lists the guide's main requirements.

The guide also says subscribers and members of a membership program keep the right to opt out of marketing emails. Among the transactional or relationship categories it lists is a notice about a change in the terms or features of a membership or subscription. As general advice, keep plan notices and offers in separate emails, so a member who opts out of offers still hears about their plan. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Maintenance plans and memberships

A plan that renews on its own, such as a yearly service agreement, can bring steady repeat work. The FTC amended its Negative Option Rule in 2024; among other things, the 2024 version required simple ways to cancel recurring charges. According to the FTC's Federal Register notice of February 12, 2026, a federal appeals court vacated that 2024 version, and the FTC restored the rule to the form it had before the 2024 version took effect. So the 2024 amendments are not in force.

This answer does not cover the restored rule's details, other federal law or state laws, which may differ; a lawyer can say what applies to your plan. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

As general advice, a plan tends to keep customers when it is easy to understand and easy to leave: say what it includes, what it costs and when it renews before the customer agrees, get a clear yes, remind people before each renewal, and let them cancel as easily as they joined.

Count the repeat work

When a returning customer books again, record the new job with the source channel direct, since a returning customer counts as direct, and follow it to an outcome: won, lost or still open. Over a few months, that shows whether these habits are working.

For customers who drifted away, see the related answer on winning back past customers; for customers who send friends your way, the related answer on referral programs.
