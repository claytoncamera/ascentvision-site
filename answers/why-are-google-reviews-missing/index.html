Why are some of my Google reviews missing or not showing up?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Google says missing reviews were usually removed for breaking its policies, such as spam, and those are not restored. Others are delayed: a new review can take a few days to be checked, and reviews from merged profiles a few days to show. A customer on an older phone may not have managed to post. Google can also pause reviews on some profiles, and its spam filter occasionally removes real ones; for that, contact Google support.

The reasons Google gives

Google's help page on missing or delayed reviews lists several possible reasons a review is not on a profile:

  • Checking: reviews are checked against Google's policies, which can take a few days.
  • A recent merge: after Business Profiles are merged, reviews from both can take a few days to show on Search and Maps.
  • An older phone: customers on older phones or software may have trouble leaving a review.
  • A policy removal: Google says "Reviews removed for policy violations won’t be restored", and that this is the usual reason.
  • A pause: Google may temporarily disable reviews for certain profiles or business categories, which it says is to protect business owners.
  • A reinstated profile: Google notes that reviews are sometimes removed after a profile is reinstated, and says to contact support if that happens.

When a real review is removed

Google uses automated spam detection, and its page on reporting reviews says that "the system may also mistakenly remove legitimate reviews". Its advice in that case is to contact support.

A restriction is a different case. Google says that if it finds a business owner broke its fake engagement policy, it may remove those reviews and add restrictions, such as blocking new reviews or unpublishing existing ones for a set period, or showing customers a warning that fake reviews were removed. Google says it emails the owner before applying one, and that the business can appeal.

What an owner can check

  • Give a brand-new review a few days.
  • Check the email linked to the profile for a notice from Google about a restriction.
  • A recent merge or reinstatement can explain a gap.
  • If a customer could not post, Google says they must be signed in to a Google Account, and recommends updating the Maps app.
  • If you believe a genuine review was removed by mistake, contact Google support; as general advice, that is usually a better first step than asking for it to be posted again.

Why a batch of re-posts can backfire

When several reviews vanish, it is tempting to ask those customers to post again, all at once. Google's policy counts "unusual volumes or patterns of review contributions" that suggest an effort to manipulate a rating as rating manipulation, and it does not allow the same content to be posted several times. It also bars asking only the customers you expect to be happy, and offering incentives in exchange for a review.

As general advice, a sudden wave of re-posted reviews, some in the same words, can look like that pattern even when every customer is real. The steadier path is to keep asking every customer as each job ends; the related answer on getting more reviews the right way covers how, and the related answer on getting a fake Google review removed covers reviews that should go.
