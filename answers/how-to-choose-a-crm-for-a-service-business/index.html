How do you choose a CRM for a service business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 30, 2026

Start with the jobs the system must do each day, not its feature list: record a new inquiry, remind you to follow up, track a quote, book the job, mark it done and ask for a review. Shortlist two, run a real week of leads through a trial of each, test the connections you need and a full export, then set who can see what. Some service businesses may find job software with a CRM built in is enough.

Start with the daily jobs

Write down what the system has to do in a normal week. The related answer on what a CRM is covers what a record holds, when a spreadsheet is enough and what to look for; this page is about the steps of choosing.

  • Record each new inquiry with where it came from.
  • Remind someone to follow up, with a date.
  • Track a quote from sent to accepted or lost.
  • Show what is booked and what is done.
  • Ask for a review, and ask every customer, with no reward.

Five steps to a decision

Take the steps in order. A tool that fails an early step may not need the later ones.

StepWhat to doWhat to look for
Shortlist twoPick two that cover your list of jobs, using each vendor's own pages.Two real trials can be easier to judge than six
Run a real weekEnter every real inquiry for a week, then compare.Whether people use it without being reminded
Test the connectionsTry the ones you need: web forms, calendar, accounting and logging of phone calls.Whether each link works on the plan you would use, and carries the source
Test a full exportExport everything during the trial and open the file.Whether notes, history and sources come out
Set rolesDecide who can edit, view, export and delete before real data goes in.Only the access each job needs; the related answer on who should have access to your CRM covers roles

CRM or job software?

If your harder problem is quotes, scheduling and invoices, job software that includes customer records may fit better than a stand-alone CRM. The related answer on field service software like Jobber or Housecall Pro compares two such products and what to check before signing.

How to judge the trial

After the week, these questions usually say more than a feature comparison.

  • Did everyone enter their own inquiries?
  • Did every record get a source and a next-step date?
  • Did every new job this week have its source recorded?
  • Was anything awkward enough that people started keeping notes elsewhere?
