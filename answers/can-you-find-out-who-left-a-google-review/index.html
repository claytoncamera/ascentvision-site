Can you find out who left a Google review?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Only what the reviewer's public profile shows. Google's Maps help says all reviews are public and none can be anonymous, so you see the name on the reviewer's About me page and their other public reviews and photos. Google's privacy policy says it does not share personal information with individuals outside Google except in cases it lists, such as consent or legal reasons. If the name fits no customer, check your records, reply calmly and report the review if it breaks Google's rules.

What Google lets you see

Google's Maps help says all reviews are public and none can be anonymous. Others can find the name on the reviewer's About me page, other photos and videos they added to Google Maps with the location info attached, and other reviews they wrote there. The related answers on reviewing without a Gmail account and on what a Google Local Guide is cover the account and the badge.

As general advice, the profile name may be a nickname rather than the name on your invoice, and the date may not be the date of the job: Google says an edited review shows the date of its last edit.

What Google keeps private

Google's Maps help says "You can't contact someone who left an inappropriate review", and points to reporting the review instead. Its privacy policy says it does not share personal information with companies, organizations or individuals outside Google except in the cases it lists, such as with the person's consent or for legal reasons. As general advice, do not expect Google to tell you who is behind a profile. Whether a review justifies legal action is a question for a lawyer.

When the name fits no customer

  • Check your records for the name, date and details; as general advice, a customer's relative may have posted it.
  • Reply once, calmly, saying you cannot find the visit and inviting them to contact you; the related answer on how to respond to a negative Google review covers the wording.
  • If it describes something that never happened, report it. Google's content policy does not allow content that is not based on a real experience, or that is based on a conflict of interest such as current or former employment or an industry competitor. The related answer on getting a fake Google review removed covers the steps.
  • If one profile keeps posting rule-breaking content, Google's reporting page describes flagging that profile in the Google Maps app.

Why tracking a reviewer down can backfire

Google's Maps content policy lists doxxing as harassment it does not allow, and bars posting another person's personal information without consent. As general advice, a reply that reveals who you think a reviewer really is can turn a review into a complaint about you.

The FTC's guidance on its consumer reviews rule says the rule bars intimidation used to get a review removed or changed, and that intimidation can include "abusive communications, stalking, character assassination" used to induce fear. It also says the rule does not stop a business contacting customers who post negative reviews to resolve the issues. This is a plain summary, not legal advice. If you recognize a real customer, use that to fix the problem, by phone or text only with the person's consent, not to press for a change.
