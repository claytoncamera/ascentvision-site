Should a service business put its time into SEO or into social media?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

For most service businesses, the first hours are usually best spent on being found where customers check before they hire: Google search, the map results and AI answers. Those people need the job done now. Social media mostly reaches people who are not looking yet, which can build familiarity and referrals but often brings work more slowly. Once search is covered, add one social platform your customers use, and judge both by the inquiries each brings in.

Two kinds of customer

Someone who types "water heater repair" into Google, or asks an assistant who to call, usually has the problem today. Someone scrolling a feed is usually not looking for help at all. Search, the map results and AI answers meet the first person; social media mostly meets the second, who may remember your name later or pass it to a friend.

What the sources say

Google's guide "Do you need an SEO?" tells the owner of a small local business that "you can probably do much of the work yourself". It also says it costs nothing to appear in Google's unpaid results.

On the social side, Pew Research Center's 2026 fact sheet says YouTube and Facebook are the most widely used platforms among U.S. adults, and that use varies by age. Among adults 65 and older, 59% said they ever use Facebook, against 21% for Instagram and 11% for TikTok. The related answer on which social media a service business should use has the full table.

A simple rule for limited time

  • First, cover the places a customer checks before hiring: a website that says what you do and where, a complete Google Business Profile and recent, real reviews. The related answer on whether SEO is worth it for a small business covers when search tends to pay off.
  • Then add one social platform your customers use, and post about real jobs on a rhythm you can keep. A second platform can wait until the first runs itself.
  • If referrals already fill the calendar, staying in front of past customers may matter more for now; the related answer on whether you need a website if you use social media covers that case.

Judge both by inquiries

Record every inquiry with its channel (for these two, usually search, AI answer or social, and paid for anything from a boosted post or ad) and follow each to won, lost or still open. Search usually builds over months, so as general advice, give it at least two quarters before comparing it with social. The related answer on how to get leads without ads covers the other channels that need no ad budget, such as referrals and partners.
