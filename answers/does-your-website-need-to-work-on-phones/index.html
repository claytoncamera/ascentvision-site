Does a small business website really need to work well on phones?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

Yes. Pew Research Center's Mobile Fact Sheet reports that about nine in ten U.S. adults (91%) own a smartphone. Often, the phone is the first place people look for help. If your website is slow, hard to read or hard to act on from a phone, many visitors will leave before they call. You can test the basics yourself in a few minutes.

How many people carry a phone

Pew Research Center's Mobile Fact Sheet, dated November 20, 2025, reports that 91% of U.S. adults own a smartphone, based on a survey run from February 5 to June 18, 2025. The same page notes that the figure was 35% in the Center's 2011 survey.

For a service business, that means a customer looking for help is very likely holding a phone, often while standing next to the problem they want fixed.

A quick phone check you can do yourself

Open your own website on your phone, as a customer would, and work through this list:

  • Tap the phone number. It should start a call without copying or retyping.
  • Fill in and send the contact form. Check that the fields are easy to tap, the keyboard fits each field, and the message actually arrives.
  • Read the home page without zooming in. Text should be large enough and fit the screen.
  • Turn off Wi-Fi and load the site on mobile data. If it takes long enough to notice, it is too slow.
  • Find your service area, hours and main services within a few seconds of scrolling.
  • Check that buttons are far enough apart that you do not hit the wrong one.
  • Look for pop-ups that cover the page and are hard to close on a small screen.

What to fix first

Start with whatever stops someone from getting in touch: a number that is not tappable, a broken form or a page that will not load. Those cost inquiries directly. Layout and look come after.

If many things fail, the site may need more than small fixes. Many older sites can be fixed; a rebuild makes sense when the site cannot be made fast on a phone without starting over.

Check it again over time

Phones, browsers and plugins update often, so a site that worked last year can break quietly. Repeating the same checklist every few months, and after any change to the site, catches most problems before customers do.

Sources

  1. Pew Research Center, "Mobile Fact Sheet" (November 20, 2025)
