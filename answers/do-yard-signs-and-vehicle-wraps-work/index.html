Do yard signs and vehicle wraps bring in customers?

Ascent Vision · Published October 25, 2026

They can, mainly by making your name familiar where the work already is: a sign outside a job in progress, a lettered van parked on the same streets every week. They rarely reach someone at the moment of an urgent search. To see whether they bring work, track each sign batch or vehicle separately and count the jobs won. Check your city's sign rules first.

What they do well

Signs and wraps advertise where your work is already happening. A neighbor walking past a job sees your name next to work they can judge for themselves, and a truck seen on the same streets every week becomes familiar. That familiarity often pays off later, when someone needs the service and remembers a name.

They tend to suit businesses that work at homes in a set area, such as roofers, painters, landscapers and pool services. They usually do less when customers rarely see the work, or when jobs are spread thinly across a wide region.

What they cannot do

A sign or a wrap cannot explain much. Most people driving past will take in a name, a trade and perhaps a phone number or short web address, so keep to those, in large type that can be read from a distance. A clever slogan rarely helps if no one can read the number.

They also work slowly. Someone may see your van in spring and call in the fall, which is why they are usually better treated as a steady background than as a quick way to fill next week.

How to tell whether they bring work

The related answer on tracking leads from flyers, signs and vehicles covers the method: a number or short web address used only on that sign batch or vehicle, the question "How did you hear about us?" for every new customer, and recording those leads under the campaign channel with an outcome of won, lost or still open.

One source it is easy to miss: a neighbor who sees your sign at a job and walks over to ask the crew. Ask your team to pass on the name and number the same day, so that inquiry is recorded with the sign and the job address in the notes.

Rules to check first

Sign rules are local. Cities and counties often limit the size of signs, where they can stand and how long they can stay up, and homeowners' associations can add rules of their own. Check your city's sign rules, and ask the customer before you put a sign in their yard.

If you are a licensed contractor, check whether your license number must appear on ads and vehicles; the related answer on showing your license and insurance covers Florida's rule.
