How do you switch marketing agencies without losing your website or leads?

Ascent Vision · Published October 15, 2026

Switch in an order that keeps you in control. First confirm that you own, and can log in to, your domain, website, hosting, analytics, ad accounts and business profiles. Then give notice. Before access ends, collect your content, lead data, phone numbers and reports. Overlap the old and new agency briefly where you can, and change passwords once the handover is done.

Before you give notice

  • Read the contract for the notice period, who owns what, and what happens to the work when it ends.
  • Check that your domain is registered in your name and that renewal is billed to you.
  • Log in yourself to every account as an owner or administrator, not only as a user the agency added.
  • Write down everything the agency runs for you, including small tools you may have forgotten.

The handover checklist

ItemWhat to secureWhy it matters
Domain nameOwner login and renewal in your nameLosing it takes your website and email with it
Hosting and websiteOwner access and a full backupSo the site stays up and can be moved
Content and filesCopy, photos, videos, logos and designsSo nothing has to be recreated
Lead dataAn export of every form, call and recordYour history of who asked and what they became
Phone numbersAny tracking numbers, transferred or forwardedOld numbers may still be printed on trucks and cards
Reports and analyticsPast reports and owner access to analyticsYour baseline for judging the new agency
Business profilesOwner access to Google, Facebook and review sitesThese carry your reviews and contact details
Ad accountsOwner access and billing in your nameKeeps your account history and settings

What to ask the old agency

  • A full list of every account and tool they manage for you, with your admin access to each.
  • An export of all lead data in a spreadsheet.
  • Whether any phone numbers, images, plugins or code are licensed to them rather than to you.
  • What will stop working on the end date because it is billed to their account.
  • A list of work in progress and anything they do on a schedule.

Timing

Avoid switching right before your busy season. Give notice only after you have confirmed your own access, and ask for a short overlap so that nothing goes dark between the two agencies.

Once the handover is complete, remove the old agency's access, change shared passwords and keep a record of where everything now lives. A clean handover usually costs a few weeks of attention, and saves months of rebuilding.
