Is Nextdoor worth it for a local service business?

Ascent Vision · Published October 24, 2026

Often, yes, for a business that serves homes in a set area. Nextdoor says its free Business Page lets a business post to nearby neighborhoods and collect recommendations from neighbors, and it offers ads as a separate option. In Pew Research Center's 2026 survey, 23% of suburban adults said they ever use Nextdoor, against 15% in cities and 11% in rural areas. Judge it by the inquiries it brings.

What a business gets on Nextdoor

Nextdoor's page "Create your Business Page on Nextdoor" says a free Business Page lets a business build an online presence, collect recommendations from neighbors and reach local customers with unlimited free posts. It also says a business can respond to customers' messages from its page.

The same page says business information on Nextdoor can come from third-party public data, or from members who recommended or commented on a business and could not find its page. So a page for your business may exist before you claim it. Nextdoor says you must be an owner, employee or other authorized representative to claim one. Nextdoor's small-business page lists ads as a separate option from the free page and free posts.

Who uses it

Pew Research Center's fact sheet "Americans' Use of Social Media in 2026" is based on a survey of 5,511 U.S. adults conducted from January 30 to June 18, 2026. These are the shares who said they ever use Nextdoor.

Recommendations are earned, not bought

Nextdoor's page on Faves, its name for a neighbor's recommendation, says every Fave comes from a verified neighbor who hired or chose the business, and that there is "No way to buy it". It suggests sharing your Fave link with your best customers.

As general advice: ask right after a job goes well, send the link by text only with the person's consent, and never offer anything in return, as the related answer on getting more reviews the right way explains.

How to tell if it is worth it

Ask every new inquiry how they found you, and record Nextdoor leads by how they arrived: a neighbor's recommendation is referral, a reply to your post or a visit to your page is social, and an ad is paid. Put Nextdoor in the notes, and follow each lead to won, lost or still open.

After a few months, the list shows whether Nextdoor brings jobs. The free page is often worth claiming either way, since neighbors may already be talking about you there; the paid ads are worth keeping only if the jobs they bring outweigh what they cost.
