What is call tracking, and can it hurt your Google listing?

Ascent Vision · Published October 26, 2026

Call tracking gives each source of calls its own phone number, such as an ad, a mailer or your Google profile, and each number rings through to your usual line, so you can see which number a caller dialed. Google lets a Business Profile show a primary number and up to 2 more, and its guidelines say the number must be under the business's direct control. Keep your main number on the profile, and own the numbers where you can.

How call tracking works

Each place you advertise gets its own number. When someone dials, the call rings at your normal phone, and the service usually logs which number was dialed, when and for how long. If a service also records calls, get the caller's consent first; the related answer on consent to record customer calls covers that.

Google Ads has its own version: its help pages describe "Google forwarding numbers" that replace your business number on your website, "without any cost to you", so you can see which ads lead to calls from the site. It counts such a call as a conversion only when it lasts longer than a minimum length you set.

What it tells you, and what it does not

A tracking number shows which number was dialed, not why. Someone who saw your truck, then searched your name, dials the number on your Google profile. So keep asking "How did you hear about us?" on every call, and log both answers; the related answer on tracking phone call leads covers what to record.

In the lead record, a number printed on a flyer, sign or vehicle points to campaign, an ad's number to paid, and the profile's number to search. Follow each call to won, lost or still open.

Can it hurt your Google listing?

Google's help page on adding a phone number says that in addition to the primary number you can add up to 2 more, and that adding a number "can improve your business's visibility on Google". Its guidelines ask for a number that connects to your business location, a local number instead of a central call center line "whenever possible", and a number "under the direct control of the business". They also bar numbers that send callers to numbers other than the business's own.

As general advice, not Google's wording: a tracking number that rings straight through to your own line, on an account the business controls, fits those words better than one routed through a vendor's call center or a lead seller. Keeping your main number on the profile as one of the extra numbers means customers who saved it still find it.

Who owns the numbers

Tracking numbers are often rented from the vendor, and a rented number can stop working, or stay with the vendor, when the service ends; the related answer on who should own your business phone number explains the risk. Before a tracking number goes on a truck or a sign, ask in writing whether the business can keep it if you leave.
