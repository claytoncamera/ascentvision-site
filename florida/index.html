Three things to plan for in Florida

Recording a call needs everyone’s consent

Florida’s law (Florida Statutes, section 934.03) allows recording a call when all of the parties to it have given prior consent. Federal law generally needs only one person on the call to agree, so Florida asks more. A lead system that records calls should say so at the start and ask. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Sales texts and calls have their own state law

Florida’s telephone solicitation law (Florida Statutes, section 501.059) counts a call, a text message or a voicemail sent to sell consumer goods or services as a telephonic sales call. An unsolicited one made with an automated dialing system or a recorded message needs the person’s prior express written consent. A call or text answering someone’s own request is treated differently, so a lead system should record what each person asked for and agreed to. Hurricane season shapes the year

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30 (NOAA’s National Hurricane Center). For many Florida service businesses, demand, scheduling and what customers ask about move with it, so the pages people find and the way inquiries are answered should be ready before the season, not during it.

Does a Florida business need a Spanish version of its website?

Only when a real share of its customers prefer Spanish and the business can serve them in Spanish, on the phone and on the job as well as on the page. Check your own area and your own inquiries first, and have a fluent person review every page rather than publishing unchecked machine translation.

Can a Florida business record calls with new leads?

Florida’s law (Florida Statutes, section 934.03) allows recording a call when all of the parties have given prior consent; federal law generally needs only one person on the call to agree. The safe habit is to tell callers at the start that the call may be recorded, ask whether that is okay, and stop if they say no. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.
